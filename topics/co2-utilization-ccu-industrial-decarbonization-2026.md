# CO2 Utilization (CCU) Pathways for Industrial Decarbonization (2026)

*Image source: Pexels*

Carbon dioxide utilization (CCU) converts captured CO2 into valuable products such as chemicals, fuels, building materials, and polymers, turning emissions into economic assets and supporting circular economy models in hard-to-abate sectors.

## Key Pathways

- **Chemical synthesis**: CO2 to methanol, urea, or polymers via catalysis and hydrogenation.
- **Mineralization**: CO2 reacted with minerals or industrial wastes to form carbonates for cement, aggregates, or construction materials.
- **Biological conversion**: Algae or microbes convert CO2 into biofuels, proteins, or specialty chemicals.
- **Electrochemical and plasma routes**: Emerging low-temperature processes for direct CO2-to-chemicals.

## Recent Developments

The European UNCOVER project (launched September 2026), coordinated by ITA with €7M Horizon Europe funding, demonstrates six CO2 conversion technologies to produce ten industrial products from emissions in steel, cement, and chemicals sectors. [I3A, 2026](http://i3a.unizar.es/en/noticias/new-european-project-will-advance-industrial-use-co2)

Shougang-Rio Tinto's industrial-scale CCS trial at Jingtang (commissioned September 2026) captures blast furnace gas, with potential CCU extensions for ethanol and fuels via LanzaTech partnership. [Mining Digital, 2026](https://miningdigital.com/articles/rio-tinto-and-shougang-commission-industrial-scale-ccs-trial)

## Integration with Existing Strategies

CCU complements carbon capture in steel and cement (see topics/carbon-capture-steel-cement.md) and insetting approaches by creating value-chain revenue streams. It aligns with EU Industrial Decarbonisation Bank and Innovation Fund priorities.

## Challenges and Outlook

Economic viability depends on cheap renewable hydrogen and policy support for CCU credits. Scalable demonstrations like UNCOVER are critical for 2030+ deployment.
