# Glass Decarbonization Pathways (Europe, 2026)

Glass melting needs continuous heat at 1,000–1,600°C, and most European furnaces still run on natural gas. A smaller but stubborn share of emissions comes from the raw materials themselves: carbonates such as soda ash and limestone release CO2 when they melt. [JRC Science for Policy Brief JRC144091 (2026)](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144091/Glass_factsheet_JRC144091_1.pdf) That split decides the toolkit. Electricity, low-carbon fuels and efficiency can tackle the combustion part, while recycled glass (cullet) and, possibly, carbon capture are the only levers for the process part.

This page draws on the European Commission's Joint Research Centre (JRC), the European glass industry bodies Glass Alliance Europe (GAE) and FEVE (container glass), and the German glass association BV Glas. Industry-body figures are the associations' own. For the JRC's short summary of emerging technologies across hard-to-abate sectors, see [EU JRC Factsheets on Emerging Technologies for Hard-to-Abate Industries (2026)](eu-emerging-technologies-hard-to-abate-industries-2026.md).

## Production and Emissions Baseline

European glass production by sector, EU27 plus the UK (thousand tonnes):

| Year | Flat glass | Container glass | Mineral wool insulation | Domestic glass | Continuous filament glass fibre | Other (incl. special glass) | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2022 | 11,700 | 22,420 | 4,850 | 1,161 | 910 | 680 | 41,721 |
| 2024 | 10,500 | 20,040 | 5,000 | 825 | 780 | 580 | 37,725 |
| 2025 | 10,000 | 20,330 | 5,148 | 795 | 760 | 460 | 37,493 |

Source: [Glass Alliance Europe, Statistical Report 2025–2026 (June 2026), pp. 5–6](https://www.wko.at/oe/industrie/glasindustrie/statistical-report-glass-alliance-europe.pdf). Figures are saleable glass, except flat glass, which is melted glass. Mineral wool has been included since 2019. In 2025 flat glass output fell 4.76% and container glass rose 1.45%. The sector had 128,500 direct employees, and extra-EU imports rose 5.5% in volume to 5.27 Mt. [GAE Statistical Report 2025–2026, pp. 3–4](https://www.wko.at/oe/industrie/glasindustrie/statistical-report-glass-alliance-europe.pdf)

- **Direct emissions**: about 22 Mt CO2e in 2022. CO2 per unit of output fell 43% between 1990 and 2020, mainly through the switch to natural gas, more cullet and efficiency gains. [JRC144091](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144091/Glass_factsheet_JRC144091_1.pdf)
- **Intensity**: 1 kg of container or flat glass melted in a gas-fired furnace generates about 0.4–0.6 kg of CO2. About 75–80% comes from fuel combustion and 20–25% from the decomposition of carbonate raw materials. [JRC144091](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144091/Glass_factsheet_JRC144091_1.pdf)
- **Energy mix**: natural gas supplies 75–85% of the sector's energy, electricity 10–15% and other fossil fuels 5–10%. Melting accounts for 80–90% of energy demand in container and flat glass. The theoretical minimum for melting soda-lime-silica glass is 2.6 GJ/t, while real furnaces average about 5.8 GJ/t for container glass and above 6.5 GJ/t for flat glass. [JRC144091](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144091/Glass_factsheet_JRC144091_1.pdf)
- **Furnace cycles set the clock**: glass furnaces last 10–20 years, so only 2–3 furnace generations can be rebuilt before 2050. Investments made this decade will largely fix the sector's emissions to 2050. [JRC144091](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144091/Glass_factsheet_JRC144091_1.pdf)

## Decarbonization Levers

The JRC rates technologies by readiness (TRL, 1–9) and CO2 abatement potential. The potentials cannot be added up, and some options exclude others.

| Lever | Technology | TRL | CO2 abatement potential |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Electrification | Electric melting, small furnaces | 9 | 75–85% |
| Electrification | Electric melting, large furnaces and specific glass types | 5 | 75–85% |
| Electrification | Electric melting, continuous filament glass fibre | 3 | 75–85% |
| Fuel switch | Biogas or biomethane | 8 | 75–85% |
| Fuel switch | Liquid biofuel | 8 | 75–85% |
| Fuel switch | Hydrogen, 20% of the gas mix | 7 | ≤20% |
| Fuel switch | Hydrogen, 100% | 5 | 75–85% |
| Circularity | More cullet, container glass | 9 | ≤20% |
| Circularity | More cullet, flat glass | 9 | ≤5% |
| Process optimisation | Oxy-fuel combustion | 7–9 | ≤50% |
| Process optimisation | Waste heat recovery | 9 | ≤15% |
| Process optimisation | Raw material preheating | 8 | ≤15% |
| Process optimisation | Batch reformulation | 4 | ≤20% |
| Process optimisation | Batch pelletisation | 6–8 | ≤5% |
| CCUS | Carbon capture, utilisation and storage | 4 | ≤90% |

Source: [JRC144091, Table 2](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144091/Glass_factsheet_JRC144091_1.pdf).

- **Electrification** is the JRC's central lever for deep decarbonisation. FEVE notes that molten glass conducts electricity, so electrodes can heat the melt more efficiently than burners. [FEVE (2026), p. 4](https://feve.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FEVE-Decarbonisation-Report-2025.pdf) Electric melting is proven in furnaces below about 200 t/day, but large furnaces (flat glass furnaces average about 700 t/day), high cullet ratios and some glass compositions still need innovation and demonstration. [JRC144091](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144091/Glass_factsheet_JRC144091_1.pdf) **Hybrid furnaces**, which combine electric melting with gas or oxy-fuel firing, are the bridge. FEVE says they can replace up to 80% of natural gas with renewable electricity. [FEVE, Accelerating the European container glass industry's energy transition (2026), p. 4](https://feve.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FEVE-Decarbonisation-Report-2025.pdf)
- **Fuel switching**: hydrogen blends above 20% require furnace redesign and raise NOx concerns, and biogas and biomethane supply is limited. [JRC144091, Table 1](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144091/Glass_factsheet_JRC144091_1.pdf)
- **Cullet** cuts both kinds of emissions, because it replaces carbonate raw materials and melts with less energy. [FEVE (2026), p. 8](https://feve.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FEVE-Decarbonisation-Report-2025.pdf) In 2023 the EU collected 80.8% of glass packaging for recycling, against a 90% target for 2030. [FEVE (2026), p. 8](https://feve.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FEVE-Decarbonisation-Report-2025.pdf) Flat glass gets far less from cullet (≤5% abatement in the JRC table), and the JRC points to the need for separate collection and coordination with the construction and automotive sectors. [JRC144091, Tables 1–2](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144091/Glass_factsheet_JRC144091_1.pdf)
- **CCUS** is the only lever for the 20–25% of emissions from raw materials, but it needs CO2 transport and storage infrastructure and is likely oversized for a typical glass site. [JRC144091, Table 1](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144091/Glass_factsheet_JRC144091_1.pdf)

The JRC summarises a suggested order of action, based on a 2025 Brussels School of Governance study. From 2025 to 2030, maximise cullet and efficiency. From 2030 to 2045, scale hybrid electric boosting where low-carbon power is affordable, and consider oxy-fuel retrofits with capture where CO2 infrastructure is planned. Around 2040, complete the move to renewable hydrogen or near-100% electric melting where infrastructure allows. [JRC144091](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144091/Glass_factsheet_JRC144091_1.pdf)

## Costs

The JRC notes that most low-carbon options have much higher operating costs than natural gas (about €50/MWh). In 2023 flat glass makers paid about €150/MWh for electricity. Fossil hydrogen cost about €6/kg (about €180/MWh) and renewable hydrogen over €10/kg (about €300/MWh). The extra capital cost of electric furnaces for container and flat glass is expected to fall from €111 per tonne of glass in 2020 to €82/t in 2050. [JRC144091](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144091/Glass_factsheet_JRC144091_1.pdf)

FEVE estimates that the container glass industry needs about €20 billion of additional capital expenditure to reach net zero by 2050. Its members invest over €600 million a year in innovation and decarbonisation, and companies producing 90% of Europe's glass packaging by tonnage have signed up to the Science Based Targets initiative. [FEVE (2026), pp. 2–3](https://feve.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FEVE-Decarbonisation-Report-2025.pdf)

They typically make under 100 t of glass a day with low cullet, while a conventional container furnace makes about 350 t/day with high cullet. ## Case Study: Germany's 2045 Roadmap (October 2026)

BV Glas updated its roadmap for the German glass industry in October 2026, using a 2024 baseline and site-level modelling. It covers hollow, flat, technical and other glass, about 95% of German production by tonnage excluding stone wool. German output was 6.66 Mt in 2024 and 6.60 Mt in 2025. [BV Glas, Roadmap 2045 Update (2026), Executive Summary and Section 1.2.3](https://www.bvglas.de/media/BV_Glas/2026-10-19-Decarbonization_Glass_2045-Update_final.pdf)

| Pathway in 2045 | CO2 (Mt/a) | Final energy (PJ/a) | Electricity (PJ/a) | Hydrogen (PJ/a) | Natural gas (PJ/a) | Cumulative plant investment 2024–2045 (€bn) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Business as usual | 2.36 | 43.8 | 7.6 | 0 | 36.2 | 1.84 |
| Electrification | 0.492 | 33.3 | 26 | 7.3 | 0 | 5.64 |
| Hydrogen | 0.492 | 38.7 | 14.5 | 24.2 | 0 | 3.76 |
| Hybrid | 0.492 | 36.8 | 24.8 | 12 | 0 | 6.15 |
| CCS | 0.344 | 35.5 | 23.1 | 2.2 | 10.2 | 6.11 |

Source: [BV Glas, Roadmap 2045 Update, Table 1](https://www.bvglas.de/media/BV_Glas/2026-10-19-Decarbonization_Glass_2045-Update_final.pdf). Total CO2 in 2024, including indirect emissions from electricity, was 3.64 Mt.

The three transformation pathways all end at 0.492 Mt CO2 a year, an 86.5% cut from 2024, and what remains is process CO2 from carbonate raw materials. Only the CCS pathway goes lower, to 0.344 Mt (−90.6%), and because CCS is assumed to start only in 2040 its cumulative contribution is limited. The authors conclude there is no single route: the right choice depends on energy demand, infrastructure, technology readiness, investment and operating costs, not just 2045 emissions. For large flat glass furnaces, even the electrification pathway keeps highly electrified hybrid furnaces rather than all-electric melting. [BV Glas, Roadmap 2045 Update, Executive Summary and electrification pathway](https://www.bvglas.de/media/BV_Glas/2026-10-19-Decarbonization_Glass_2045-Update_final.pdf)

## Policy Context

- Glass is treated as at risk of carbon leakage under the EU ETS. It is not covered by the first phase of CBAM, though its possible inclusion is being discussed. [JRC144091](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144091/Glass_factsheet_JRC144091_1.pdf) See [EU CBAM: Definitive Period from 2026](../policies/eu-cbam-definitive-period-2026.md) and [EU Carbon Market Reform](../policies/eu-carbon-market-reform-2026.md).
- **State aid for hybrid furnaces**: in September 2026 Glass Alliance Europe and FEVE asked that the revised General Block Exemption Regulation (GBER) for 2027–2034 drop provisions (Articles 51(3b), 52(3b) and 55(4e) of the draft) that could rule out block-exempted aid for hybrid furnaces because they still burn some fossil fuel. [FEVE, 28 September 2026](https://feve.org/glass-alliance-europe-calls-for-gber-to-support-glass-industry-decarbonisation/)

## Practical Takeaways

- **Plan around rebuild dates.** With 10–20 year furnace campaigns, the technology chosen at each rebuild from now on largely sets a site's 2050 emissions.
- **Hybrid is the near-term default for big furnaces.** All-electric melting is commercial below about 200 t/day; large container and flat glass furnaces are moving through hybrid designs first.
- **Power price and grid capacity decide the business case.** In 2023 flat glass makers paid about three times as much per MWh for electricity as for gas (our arithmetic from the JRC's €150 and €50 figures), and the JRC lists grid stability and electricity cost among the main constraints.
- **Cullet quality is a supply-chain issue.** More container cullet depends on collection and sorting; flat glass needs better recovery from construction and demolition.
- **Process CO2 needs its own answer.** Even fully electrified or hydrogen-fired furnaces leave carbonate emissions (about 0.49 Mt a year in BV Glas's German pathways), which only more cullet, decarbonated raw materials or capture can address.
