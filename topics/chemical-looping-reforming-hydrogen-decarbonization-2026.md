# Chemical Looping Reforming for Low-Carbon Hydrogen Production (2026)

Chemical looping reforming (CLR) is an emerging technology that integrates hydrogen production with built-in carbon capture, offering high efficiency for industrial decarbonization in hard-to-abate sectors like chemicals, refining, and steelmaking.

## Overview and Recent Advances

A October 2026 study from the University of Manchester provides the first plant-wide assessment of CLR, evaluating designs from small 2 MW decentralized plants to 600 MW facilities. Advanced process designs achieve thermal efficiencies up to 75% while capturing 90–99% of CO₂ emissions, with potential for excess steam export. [University of Manchester via TechXplore, Oct 2026](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-10-generation-hydrogen-production-built-carbon.html)

CLR uses metal oxide carriers in a looping process to reform methane/steam into syngas while inherently separating CO₂, reducing the energy penalty compared to post-combustion capture.

## Relevance to Industrial Decarbonization

Hydrogen is critical for sectors difficult to electrify. CLR supports low-carbon H₂ supply chains with high capture rates, complementing existing topics on green hydrogen for steel and CCUS in cement/chemicals. It addresses efficiency and integration challenges in clusters and supply chains.

## Key Benefits and Challenges

- **Benefits**: High efficiency (up to 75%), near-complete CO₂ capture, scalability across plant sizes, steam co-production.
- **Challenges**: Material durability for oxygen carriers, scale-up from TRL 4–6 to commercial, integration with existing infrastructure.

Further research and pilots are needed for widespread deployment by 2030–2035.
