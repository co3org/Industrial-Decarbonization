# Steel Decarbonization Pathways and Technologies

This document outlines the status quo, key constraints, and leading decarbonization pathways for the steel industry, drawing on IEA, World Steel Association, and recent policy developments.

## Status Quo
- Global crude steel production ~1.8-2 billion tonnes annually.
- Emissions: ~7-9% of global CO2, primarily from blast furnace-basic oxygen furnace (BF-BOF) processes using coal.
- ~70% of production via BF-BOF; electric arc furnaces (EAF) limited by scrap availability.

## Key Decarbonization Pathways
- **Hydrogen-based Direct Reduced Iron (H2-DRI-EAF)**: Replace coal with green hydrogen; IEA targets 76% EAF by 2050.
- **Carbon Capture, Utilization, and Storage (CCUS)**: Retrofit existing BF-BOF; see related [carbon-capture-steel-cement.md](carbon-capture-steel-cement.md).
- **Electrification and Scrap Recycling**: Expand EAF with renewable power.
- **Material Efficiency and Circularity**: Increase scrap use and lightweight design.

## Recent Developments
- Green steel projects facing delays; only ~$20B committed vs. $1.5T needed globally [Reuters, June 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/environment/steel-industry-sounds-alarm-over-slow-progress-green-steel-2026-06-19/).
- Pipeline would deliver only ~70 Mt green steel/year by 2030 vs. 2 Bt total production.

## Policy and Investment Enablers
- EU Clean Industrial Deal, US DOE funding, and emerging carbon pricing mechanisms.
- Need for offtake agreements and lead markets to de-risk investments.
