# Lime Decarbonization Pathways (Europe, 2026)

Lime is made by heating limestone above about 900°C until it breaks down into quicklime and CO2 (CaCO3 + heat = CaO + CO2). [EuLA, A Pathway to Negative CO2 Emissions by 2050, p. 12](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf) That chemical step, not the fuel, is the main source of the sector's emissions: process CO2 from limestone was on average 69% of direct kiln emissions in 2019. [EuLA roadmap, p. 13](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf) Clean fuels can only deal with the other 31%. The rest needs carbon capture, which makes lime one of the clearest cases of "hard-to-abate" process emissions in European industry.

This page draws mainly on the 2023 climate roadmap of the European Lime Association (EuLA), checked against official EU sources and company and project pages. Lime is not one of the seven sectors in the JRC's 2026 industrial decarbonisation factsheets, so there is no JRC figure set to compare with here. [SETIS, Industrial decarbonisation factsheets](https://setis.ec.europa.eu/publications-and-documents/industrial-decarbonisation-factsheets_en) Roadmap figures are the association's own estimates and assumptions. In the roadmap and on this page, "lime" covers both quicklime (CaO) and dolime (CaO.MgO). [EuLA roadmap, p. 3](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)

## Sector Profile

- **Size**: EuLA members (national associations and companies in 24 European countries) sold 18.2 million tonnes of lime products in 2019. [EuLA roadmap, p. 1](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)
- **Uses**: lime goes into steelmaking, construction materials, civil engineering, flue gas cleaning, drinking and waste water treatment, chemicals, non-ferrous metals, pulp and paper, and agriculture. [EuLA roadmap, pp. 2, 7](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf) Many of the sectors that buy lime are trying to decarbonise themselves, so low-carbon lime feeds into their own footprints.
- **Kilns**: there are six general kiln categories: rotary kilns with preheater, long rotary kilns, parallel flow regenerative kilns, annular shaft kilns, mixed feed shaft kilns and others. The average thermal efficiency of European lime production is 88%. [EuLA roadmap, p. 13](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf) The theoretical heat needed to break down calcium limestone is 3.2 GJ per tonne of lime. [EuLA roadmap, p. 12](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)

## Emissions Baseline

- **Direct emissions**: kilns run by EuLA members emitted 19.5 Mt of CO2 in 2019. [EuLA roadmap, p. 13](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)
- **Split**: 69% process CO2 from limestone and 31% from fuel combustion. EuLA notes that switching from solid fossil fuels to natural gas lowers the CO2 intensity of combustion, and that the sector has been using more waste-derived and biomass-derived fuels over the past decades. [EuLA roadmap, p. 13](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)
- **Electricity**: the sector used about 900 GWh of electricity in 2019, or about 250,000 t of indirect (scope 2) CO2. [EuLA roadmap, p. 20](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)
- **Carbonation in use**: lime slowly takes CO2 back from the air during its use (CaO + CO2 = CaCO3). A Politecnico di Milano study commissioned by EuLA estimates that on average 33% of the process CO2 from lime production is permanently re-absorbed during the use phase (based on 2018 sales by application), with 95% of this happening within the first year. EuLA deducts this from direct emissions when it reports the sector's net footprint. [EuLA roadmap, p. 22](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf) This accounting is EuLA's own: it still calls for carbonation to be recognised in emission inventories and carbon footprint methods. [EuLA roadmap, p. 28](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)

## Decarbonization Levers

| Lever | EuLA's 2030 milestone | EuLA's 2050 goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kiln shift and upgrade, digital tools | Deliver 2.5% of 2019 direct emissions, about half of the 4.5% remaining potential | Full 4.5% potential; 5–10% of lime production in fully electric kilns |
| Fuel switch | Alternative fuels cover half of kiln energy (about 35 PJ), including one third biomass-derived and 15% waste-derived fuels; first hydrogen and e-fuel use | 100% alternative fuels (about 60 PJ), split evenly between biomass-derived fuels, hydrogen/e-fuels and non-biomass waste-derived fuels (about 20 PJ each) |
| Carbon capture, use and storage | Capture 5–10% of direct kiln CO2, about 1.3 Mt | Capture unavoidable process CO2, about 15.5 Mt |
| Carbonation | Ongoing: 33% of process CO2 re-absorbed in use | A further 12% through "enhanced" carbonation with lime users |

Sources: [EuLA roadmap, pp. 15–19, 22–23, 27–28](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf).

- **Efficiency is mostly used up.** Most European lime is already made in kilns with a thermal efficiency above 88%, so kiln upgrades have only a few percent left to give. [EuLA roadmap, pp. 2, 15](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)
- **Fuel access depends on location.** Waste-derived fuels are easier to get in densely populated regions, where competition for them is also higher, while biomass is easier to source in remote areas. Hydrogen and e-fuels are being tested on lime kilns, with early applications possible by the end of this decade. [EuLA roadmap, p. 16](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)
- **Capture is the main lever.** EuLA calls carbon capture indispensable because of the process share. The roadmap assumes a 90% capture rate and expected capture technologies to be ready for industrial scale in 2025–2028. It sees CO2 use as a complement to storage, not an alternative, because use is not expected to deliver reductions on the same scale. [EuLA roadmap, p. 18](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)
- **Power demand rises sharply.** Capture raises electricity use per tonne of lime by a factor of around 10. EuLA expects the sector's electricity use to rise 80% by 2030 and about tenfold by 2050, to about 10 TWh. [EuLA roadmap, p. 20](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)
- **BECCS**: a kiln burning biomass whose CO2 is captured and stored removes CO2 from the atmosphere, which EuLA calls a "carbon removal pump". [EuLA roadmap, p. 19](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)

## Trajectory

- **2030**: about 20% lower direct CO2 emissions than in 2019, mainly through fuel switching and kiln upgrades, followed by the first capture-ready kilns. [EuLA roadmap, p. 5](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)
- **2050**: net negative emissions. With full capture, natural carbonation and BECCS, EuLA estimates the sector could permanently remove about 5 Mt of CO2 a year. [EuLA roadmap, pp. 6, 34](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)
- **Assumptions**: the pathway assumes stable European sales volumes from 2019 to 2050. It is a European average and cannot be applied to a single company, region or country, because local rules, energy access and infrastructure differ. [EuLA roadmap, p. 3](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)
- **Five prerequisites**: access to zero-rated biomass, green hydrogen and waste-derived fuels; multi-modal CO2 transport; affordable decarbonised electricity; permanent CO2 storage and use capacity; and recognition of CO2 removal by lime carbonation. [EuLA roadmap, pp. 5, 27–28](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf) EuLA stresses that CO2 transport, aggregation and intermediate storage must also reach inland plants, not only coastal ones. [EuLA roadmap, p. 28](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf)

## Industrial Projects

| CalCC (Lhoist, Air Liquide) | Réty, France | Air Liquide's fully electric cryogenic Cryocap FG capture on an existing lime plant; pipeline to a terminal in Dunkirk, shipping and permanent offshore storage in the North Sea, linked to the D'Artagnan pipeline/terminal and Eqiom's K6 cement project | Studying capture and storage of more than 600,000 t CO2 a year, over 80% of the site's emissions. FEED and permitting since 2022, supported by the Innovation Fund and the Connecting Europe Facility. | LEOPARD (Carmeuse) | Aisemont, Belgium | Retrofitted kilns that concentrate CO2 in the flue gas, a membrane capture unit and bioenergy (BECCS); Carmeuse says the technology relies only on electrical energy | Expected to avoid over 70,000 t CO2 a year at the site and remove more through BECCS. Selected by the Innovation Fund in November 2025. | Butterfly (Carmeuse and partners) | Seilles, Belgium | Parallel flow regenerative oxyfuel kiln that concentrates CO2 inside the kiln instead of capturing it downstream | Demonstrator construction began in June 2023. In the March–April 2026 campaign it reached more than 80% CO2 in the flue gas with good lime quality. The subsidised phase ran from mid-2022 to June 2026, and Carmeuse aims to be capture-technology ready by 2028. [Carmeuse](https://www.carmeuse.com/eu-en/co2-roadmap/butterfly) |

EuLA's roadmap lists further sector projects, including hydrogen trials (British Lime Association), Singleton Birch's oxyfuel calciner pilot, the LEILAC indirect-calcination projects (Lhoist and Tarmac), Nordkalk's modular membrane capture, and CO2 use projects such as Carmeuse's Columbus power-to-methane and Lhoist's CO2ncrEAT construction blocks. [EuLA roadmap, pp. 29–33](https://eula.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WEB_EULA-2030-Climate-Roadmap_Lime-Acts_A4_v19.pdf) These descriptions date from 2023 and come from the association, so check each project's current status before relying on it.

## Policy Context

- **EU ETS**: lime production and the calcination of dolomite or magnesite in kilns with a capacity above 50 tonnes a day are covered by the EU Emissions Trading System. [Directive 2003/87/EC, Annex I (consolidated text)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02003L0087-20240301) For 2021–2025 the free allocation benchmark was 0.725 allowances per tonne of lime and 0.815 per tonne of dolime (sintered dolime: 1.406). [Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/447, Annex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32021R0447) For the 2026 review proposal, see [EU Carbon Market Reform](../policies/eu-carbon-market-reform-2026.md).
- **CBAM**: lime is **not** a CBAM good. CBAM currently applies to cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen. [European Commission, CBAM](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en) See [EU CBAM: Definitive Period from 2026](../policies/eu-cbam-definitive-period-2026.md).
- **Funding**: the Innovation Fund, financed by the EU ETS, backs both CalCC and LEOPARD (above). See also [EU Innovation Fund 2026 Clean Industry Projects](../policies/eu-innovation-fund-2026-clean-industry-projects.md).

## Practical Takeaways

- **Start from the 69/31 split.** About two thirds of lime CO2 comes from the limestone, so fuel switching, electrification and efficiency together can at best address about a third of direct emissions.
- **Capture is the core lever, and location matters.** The first large project (CalCC) depends on a shared pipeline and port terminal. Inland plants need CO2 transport they can reach.
- **Plan for much more power.** Capture alone raises electricity use per tonne of lime about tenfold, so grid connection and power price are as important as capture technology.
- **Treat carbonation claims with care.** The 33% re-absorption figure is an industry-commissioned estimate and not yet recognised in emission inventories, so it should not be netted off without saying so.

## Open Questions

- Will CalCC start up on time in 2028, and will the shared Dunkirk CO2 infrastructure be ready?
- How will smaller, inland lime plants reach CO2 transport and storage?
- Will EU emission accounting or the carbon removal certification framework recognise CO2 uptake by lime carbonation?
- Should lime be considered for CBAM, given its high process emissions and its role as an input to steel and other CBAM sectors?
