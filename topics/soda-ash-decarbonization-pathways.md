# Soda Ash Decarbonization Pathways (Europe, 2026)

Soda ash (sodium carbonate, Na2CO3) is a basic chemical used mainly to make glass. In Europe it is made almost entirely by the Solvay (ammonia-soda) process from salt brine and limestone, because Europe has no trona deposits to mine. [JRC, LVIC-S BREF (2007), p. iv](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/lvic-s_bref_0907.pdf) The process burns limestone in a lime kiln, normally fired with coke, and also needs a lot of steam, which many plants have traditionally raised in coal-fired boilers. [JRC, LVIC-S BREF, pp. 43, 53](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/lvic-s_bref_0907.pdf) [Solvay, Climate change](https://www.solvay.com/en/sustainability/planet-progress/climate) So the sector has two separate problems to solve: the fuel for steam and power, and the CO2 chemistry of the lime kiln.

This page draws on the EU reference document for the sector (the JRC's Best Available Techniques Reference Document, or BREF, for Large Volume Inorganic Chemicals - Solids and Others, adopted in 2007), the industry association ESAPA, EU ETS legislation and company announcements. The BREF figures describe plants in the mid-2000s and are the most recent official EU ranges; company figures are the companies' own estimates.

## Sector Profile

- **Size in Europe**: members of the European Soda Ash Producers Association (ESAPA, a Cefic sector group) run 12 soda ash plants in six EU countries (Bulgaria, France, Germany, Italy, Poland, Spain) and Turkey, with a combined capacity of over 10 million tonnes a year, up to 15% of world production. [ESAPA](https://www.esapa.eu/)
- **Uses**: glass, detergents, chemicals such as sodium bicarbonate and silicates, and more. [ESAPA](https://www.esapa.eu/) Glass accounts for more than half of world soda ash use. [Solvay, Inventing the Solvay soda ash process... again](https://www.solvay.com/en/article/inventing-soda-ash-again) Newer demand comes from solar glass and lithium carbonate for batteries. [Solvay, Soda ash is invisible, essential, yet critical (28 Sep 2026)](https://www.solvay.com/en/article/invisible-material-will-power-energy-transition)
- **Global picture**: about 71 million tonnes were produced worldwide in 2025, 52 Mt synthetic and 19 Mt from natural trona, and China made about half. [Solvay, 28 Sep 2026, citing Chemical Market Analytics](https://www.solvay.com/en/article/invisible-material-will-power-energy-transition) Trona-based producers in the United States and Turkey avoid the Solvay process chemistry altogether.
- **Closures**: Solvay notes that the UK, Portugal and Romania no longer have soda ash plants and that Poland went from two plants to one. [Solvay, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.solvay.com/en/article/invisible-material-will-power-energy-transition) Qemetica finished shutting down ("hibernating") soda production at Janikowo, Poland, in October 2025, citing regulatory requirements, cost pressure and competition from Turkish producers. [Qemetica, 22 Oct 2025 (Polish)](https://qemetica.com/o-nas/aktualnosci/biznes/qemetica-pomyslnie-sfinalizowala-proces-hibernacji-zakladu-w-janikowie)

## Emissions and Energy Baseline

All ranges below are from the 2007 BREF, per tonne of dense soda ash.

| Item | Range | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total energy use | 9.7–13.6 GJ/t (8.8–12.8 GJ/t for light soda ash) | [BREF, p. 101](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/lvic-s_bref_0907.pdf) |
| of which lime kiln fuel | 2.2–2.8 GJ/t | [BREF, p. 53](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/lvic-s_bref_0907.pdf) |
| of which other fuels and electricity (steam, power, densification) | 7.5–10.8 GJ/t, including 50–130 kWh/t of electricity | [BREF, p. 53](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/lvic-s_bref_0907.pdf) |
| CO2 vented from the process | 200–400 kg/t | [BREF, pp. 53, 101](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/lvic-s_bref_0907.pdf) |
| CO2 bound in the product | about 415 kg/t | [BREF, p. 82](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/lvic-s_bref_0907.pdf) |

- **Why the kiln emits**: in theory the Solvay process is CO2-neutral, because the CO2 from burning limestone ends up in the product. In practice extra CO2 is needed to make up for incomplete absorption and losses, and this excess comes from burning (normally) coke in the kiln. [BREF, p. 43](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/lvic-s_bref_0907.pdf) Kilns are run to give a gas of about 40–42% CO2, and coke, coal or lignite are generally used because of the high CO2 concentration needed. [BREF, pp. 43, 85](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/lvic-s_bref_0907.pdf)
- **CO2 in the product is not stored**: the roughly 415 kg CO2/t held in soda ash is mostly released again downstream, for example when glass is melted, so soda ash is not a permanent CO2 sink. [BREF, p. 82](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/lvic-s_bref_0907.pdf)
- **Bicarbonate integration**: plants that also make refined sodium bicarbonate reuse part of the kiln CO2, which the BREF says can lead to much lower emission levels at site. [BREF, pp. 82, 101](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/lvic-s_bref_0907.pdf)
- **Steam is the bigger energy block**: about three quarters or more of total energy use goes to steam, power and densification rather than the kiln (7.5–10.8 of 9.7–13.6 GJ/t, per the ranges above). This is why boiler fuel switching is the first lever in company roadmaps.

## Decarbonization Levers

| Lever | What it addresses | Status in Europe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coal exit in site boilers (biomass, waste wood, refuse-derived fuel) | Steam and power, the largest energy use | Rheinberg done (2024); Dombasle, Laneuveville, Torrelavega and Inowrocław under way (see below) |
| Energy efficiency and kiln optimisation | Fuel use per tonne; excess kiln CO2 | Ongoing; the BREF sets the 200–400 kg CO2/t range for an optimised process |
| Reuse of CO2 in bicarbonate | Part of the kiln CO2 | At plants with bicarbonate units |
| New process chemistry (e.g. e.Solvay) | Replaces the lime kiln function | Pilot stage |
| Carbon capture | Remaining kiln CO2 | No announced European soda ash project found for this page |

## Industrial Projects

### Fuel switch at existing plants

- **Rheinberg, Germany (Solvay)**: a second biomass boiler was inaugurated in November 2024, and the site now runs on waste wood from industrial and demolition residues instead of coal, cutting CO2 by 65% (480 kt a year) compared with 2021. The cogeneration system produces steam and electricity at over 90% efficiency. [Solvay, 21 Nov 2024](https://www.solvay.com/en/press-release/solvay-renewable-energy-rheinberg-biomass-boiler-co2-reduction)
- **Dombasle, France (Solvay with Veolia, "Dombasle Énergie")**: three coal boilers are being replaced by a cogeneration unit burning 350 kt a year of refuse-derived fuel (RDF), with 181 MW thermal and 17.5 MW electrical capacity, a EUR 225 million investment, ending imports of 200,000 t of coal a year. It was first scheduled for 2024. [Solvay, 16 Feb 2022](https://www.solvay.com/en/press-release/solvay-and-veolia-launch-energy-transition-project-dombasle-energie) Solvay now expects completion by the end of 2026, cutting 240 kt of CO2 a year, or 35% of the site's 2021 emissions. [Solvay, Climate change](https://www.solvay.com/en/sustainability/planet-progress/climate) [Solvay, Dombasle RDF factsheet (Sep 2025)](https://www.solvay.com/sites/solvay_solvay/files/2025-10/Factsheet%20-%20RDF%20in%20Dombasle%20Energy.pdf)
- **Laneuveville-devant-Nancy, France (Humens/Novacarb with SUEZ, "Novasteam")**: a 55 MW boiler fired with solid recovered fuel made from about 140,000 t a year of non-recyclable waste is to supply more than 350 GWh of heat to the Novacarb soda ash plant and end its use of coal. SUEZ expected the boiler to start in 2026. [SUEZ, 18 Dec 2024 (French)](https://www.suez.com/fr/actualites/communiques-de-presse/banque-territoires-rejoint-suez-capital-novasteam-unite-valorisation-energetique-csr-accelerer-transition-environnementale-site-historique-humens)
- **Torrelavega, Spain (Solvay with ENSO)**: ENSO will build and run what is to be Spain's largest biomass cogeneration unit, replacing 70% of the plant's coal use with residues sourced within 100 km and nearly halving the site's CO2 by 2027. It was backed through Spain's Energy Saving Certificates scheme. [Solvay, 5 Mar 2025](https://www.solvay.com/en/press-release/solvay-nearly-halve-co2-emissions-torrelavega-plant-2027-major-biomass-project) Solvay has since cut capacity at Torrelavega because of global oversupply and high European energy and carbon costs. [Solvay, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.solvay.com/en/article/invisible-material-will-power-energy-transition)
- **Inowrocław, Poland (Qemetica)**: in August 2026 Qemetica signed a PLN 300 million contract to convert a coal boiler to 100% biomass (mainly wood chips), cutting coal use by about 100,000 t and CO2 by about 200,000 t a year. Commissioning is planned for the second half of 2028, with a PLN 99.9 million Modernisation Fund grant through Poland's NFOŚiGW. It is part of a PLN 2 billion transformation of Qemetica's soda business that also includes a waste-to-energy plant. [Qemetica, 13 Aug 2026](https://qemetica.com/en/about-us/news/media-i-sponsoring/the-energy-transition-in-poland-s-chemical-industry-qemetica-signs-a-300-million-pln-boiler-conversion-contract-with-mostostal-zabrze-realizacje-przemyslowe-and-sumitomo-shi-fw)
- **Devnya, Bulgaria (Solvay Sodi)**: Solvay started replacing petcoke with biomass at the end of 2022 but says its target of a full coal exit by 2030 is at risk because it cannot secure reliable local renewable fuel in the mid to long term. [Solvay, Climate change](https://www.solvay.com/en/sustainability/planet-progress/climate) In April 2026 the company presented plans for a new boiler costing about EUR 20 million, linked to a Modernisation Fund application with EIB support. [BTA, 4 Apr 2026](https://www.bta.bg/en/news/economy/1099726-energy-minister-solvay-sodi-s-management-discuss-eur-20-mln-investment-in-decar) Solvay's 2025 annual report names small modular reactors among the long-term options for this site. [Solvay, Annual Integrated Report 2025](https://www.solvay.com/sites/solvay_solvay/files/2026-03/Solvay_Annual%20Integrated%20Report_2025_0.pdf)

### New process: e.Solvay

- **What it is**: an electrochemical process, to be powered by renewable energy instead of fossil fuels, that replaces the lime kiln function; ammonia is recovered without CO2 emissions. [Solvay, Inventing the Solvay soda ash process... again](https://www.solvay.com/en/article/inventing-soda-ash-again)
- **Claimed gains** compared with the current process: 50% less CO2, 20% less energy, water and salt, 30% less limestone, and far less limestone residue. [Solvay, Inventing the Solvay soda ash process... again](https://www.solvay.com/en/article/inventing-soda-ash-again)
- **Status**: Solvay has spent about EUR 40 million on the technology (first patented in 2014) and launched a pilot at Dombasle in 2022, with plans to roll it out across its plants over about 30 years if the pilot confirms it works. [Solvay, 6 Sep 2022](https://www.solvay.com/en/press-release/solvay-reinventing-its-soda-ash-process-and-targets-zero-limestone-discharge-2050) A pilot-scale core module is now operating at Solvay's soda ash R&I facilities. [Solvay, Inventing the Solvay soda ash process... again](https://www.solvay.com/en/article/inventing-soda-ash-again) The next step is an industrial prototype of more than 10,000 t, and Solvay says it depends on low-carbon electricity at a comparable price in every European country where it operates, which does not yet exist. [Solvay, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.solvay.com/en/article/invisible-material-will-power-energy-transition)

## Policy Context

- **EU ETS**: soda ash and sodium bicarbonate production is an EU ETS activity with its own product benchmark. From 2026, intermediary CO2 (the CO2 bound in the product) is added to the soda ash benchmark, which is why the 2026–2030 value (1.122 allowances/t) is not comparable with earlier values. See [EU ETS Free-Allocation Benchmarks 2026-2030](../policies/eu-ets-benchmarks-2026-2030.md) for the values and legal sources.
- **CBAM**: soda ash is **not** a CBAM good. CBAM currently applies to cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen. [European Commission, CBAM](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en) European producers point to competition from Turkish trona producers with a much lighter carbon constraint. [Solvay, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.solvay.com/en/article/invisible-material-will-power-energy-transition) See [EU CBAM: Definitive Period from 2026](../policies/eu-cbam-definitive-period-2026.md).
- **Funding**: Modernisation Fund money (Poland, and an application in Bulgaria) and national schemes (Spain's Energy Saving Certificates) are backing boiler conversions (see projects above).

## Practical Takeaways

- **Fix the boilers first.** Steam and power are most of the energy use, and the projects that are actually being built are coal-to-biomass or coal-to-waste-fuel boiler switches.
- **Fuel supply is the limit.** Waste wood, RDF and forest residues are local and finite; Devnya shows that a coal exit can stall when reliable biomass is not available.
- **The kiln is the hard part.** Vented process CO2 of 200–400 kg/t remains after a boiler switch; the only announced route to remove it is new chemistry (e.Solvay), still at pilot stage.
- **Watch the accounting.** From 2026 the ETS benchmark counts the CO2 bound in the product, so year-on-year comparisons of soda ash intensity need care.

## Open Questions

- Will Dombasle Énergie and Novasteam start up by the end of 2026 as planned?
- Can e.Solvay reach industrial scale, and will low-carbon electricity be cheap enough to run it in Europe?
- How will plants without access to reliable biomass, such as Devnya, exit coal?
- Should soda ash be considered for CBAM, given import competition and the planned phase-out of free allocation?
- Will the BREF data (2007) be updated to reflect today's plants?
