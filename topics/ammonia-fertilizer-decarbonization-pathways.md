# Ammonia and Nitrogen Fertilizer Decarbonization Pathways (2026)

Ammonia (NH3) is the starting point for all mineral nitrogen fertilisers, and about 70% of global ammonia goes into fertilisers. The rest is used in industry for products such as plastics, explosives and synthetic fibres. [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap (Oct 2021), Executive Summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary) It is one of the most emissions-intensive commodities in heavy industry. Production relies heavily on fossil fuels, used both as feedstock and as process energy. [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)

This page complements [Chemical Industry Decarbonization Pathways and Technologies](chemical-industry-decarbonization.md), which covers the wider chemical sector.

## Emissions and Energy Baseline

The figures below come from the IEA's 2021 roadmap and describe the sector around 2020. The roadmap's mass-flow data use 2019 production. [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap, Executive Summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)

| Metric | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Share of global total final energy consumption | ~2% (8.6 EJ) |
| Share of that energy used as feedstock (supplying part of the hydrogen in the product) | ~40% |
| Production route | Just over 70% natural-gas steam reforming; most of the rest coal gasification |
| Fossil fuel demand | 170 bcm natural gas (20% of industrial gas demand); 75 Mtce coal (5% of industrial coal demand) |
| Direct CO2 emissions | ~450 Mt CO2 per year |
| Indirect CO2 emissions (electricity generation plus CO2 released when urea is applied to soils) | ~170 Mt CO2 per year |
| Direct emissions intensity | ~2.4 t CO2 per t NH3, nearly twice that of crude steel and four times that of cement |
| Average energy intensity (net) | ~41 GJ/t, against best available technology (BAT) levels of 28 GJ/t (gas-based) and 36 GJ/t (coal-based) |

Source for every row: [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap, Executive Summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)

- **Geography**: China accounts for 30% of production and 45% of the sector's CO2 emissions, and coal accounts for around 85% of its production. The United States, the EU, India, Russia and the Middle East account for a further 8–10% each. [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)
- **Trade**: global ammonia exports equal about 10% of production. Urea, its most common derivative, is traded more widely, at just under 30% of production. [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)
- **Lock-in**: plants can run for up to 50 years. European plants average around 40 years old, Chinese plants around 12. The existing global fleet could emit up to 15.5 Gt CO2 over its remaining lifetime. [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)
- **Recent trend**: the emissions intensity of ammonia has fallen by 1.1% a year over the last 10 years, mainly through energy efficiency. [IEA Breakthrough Agenda Report 2025, Fertilisers](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/fertilisers)

### Production Is Only Part of the Fertilizer Footprint

- Around 60–70% of fertiliser-related GHG emissions occur when fertilisers are used, and the rest during production. In total, fertilisers emit around 1.23 Gt CO2-equivalent per year worldwide. [IEA Breakthrough Agenda Report 2025, Fertilisers](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/fertilisers)
- Nitrogen fertiliser production also releases nitrous oxide (N2O), as well as CO2. [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)
- In a net-zero scenario from the International Fertilizer Association, emissions from fertiliser use fall by around 70% by 2050. This requires global nitrogen use efficiency (NUE) to rise from 55% to 70%, along with inhibitors, crop rotation and soil sequestration. [IEA Breakthrough Agenda Report 2025, Fertilisers](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/fertilisers)

## Decarbonization Pathways

1. **Efficiency and BAT retrofits**: closing the gap between the ~41 GJ/t global average and the 28–36 GJ/t BAT levels. In the IEA's Sustainable Development and Net Zero scenarios, BAT, operational improvements and a structural shift in processes cut average energy intensity by around 25% by 2050. On their own, these measures are not enough. [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)
2. **Fossil-based production with CCS ("blue" ammonia)**: CO2 separation is already an inherent part of commercial ammonia production, but permanent storage of that CO2 is not yet widely adopted. [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)
3. **Electrolytic hydrogen ("green" ammonia)**: water electrolysis powered by low-emission electricity replaces gas or coal as the hydrogen source. Electrolysis-based ammonia has already been run at scale using high-load-factor electricity. Using hydrogen from variable solar and wind directly in captive plants remains a challenge. [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)
4. **Methane pyrolysis**: an emerging near-zero-emission route. [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)
5. **Demand-side efficiency**: more efficient fertiliser application and more recycling of ammonia-derived plastics make 2050 production in the IEA's Sustainable Development and Net Zero scenarios 10% lower than under current policies. [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)

### Cost Gap

- **2021 estimate**: near-zero-emission routes are typically 10–100% more expensive per tonne than conventional routes, depending on energy prices and region. [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary)
- **2025 estimate** (2024 energy prices, excluding policies such as CO2 pricing): on average about 30% more using CCS and about three times more using electrolysis than conventional routes. In India's green ammonia auction run by the Solar Energy Corporation of India (SECI), a historic low price of USD 641/t was achieved. [IEA Breakthrough Agenda Report 2025, Fertilisers](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/fertilisers)

### What a Paris-Aligned Pathway Requires (IEA)

| | Sustainable Development Scenario | Net Zero Emissions by 2050 Scenario |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct CO2 emissions in 2050 vs today | more than 70% lower | 95% lower |
| Near-zero-emission share of 2050 production | nearly 70% | nearly 95% |
| Gas with CCS share of 2050 production | ~20% | ~20% |
| Electrolysis share of 2050 production | more than 25% | more than 40% |
| Annual capital investment to 2050 | USD 14 billion | USD 15 billion |

Source: [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap, Executive Summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary). The Sustainable Development Scenario alone needs more than 110 GW of electrolysers and 90 Mt of CO2 transport and storage capacity by 2050. Nearly 60% of its cumulative emission reductions come from technologies that are still at the demonstration stage.

**Pipeline reality check**: low-emission ammonia projects for fertilisers total 19 Mt, less than 10% of total production. That figure counts operational projects plus those that have reached final investment decision (FID) or completed feasibility studies. [IEA Breakthrough Agenda Report 2025, Fertilisers](https://www.iea.org/reports/breakthrough-agenda-report-2025/fertilisers)

## Operating Reference Projects

| Project | Route | Key figures | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Yara, Herøya (Porsgrunn), Norway; opened 10 June 2024 | 24 MW PEM electrolysis replacing natural gas feedstock | ~10,000 kg H2/day, enough for 20,500 t NH3/yr; cuts 41,000 t CO2/yr from the site | [Yara](https://www.yara.com/corporate-releases/yara-opens-renewable-hydrogen-plant-a-major-milestone/); [Linde Engineering](https://www.linde-engineering.com/news-and-events/press-releases/2024/yara-inaugurates-24-mw-green-hydrogen-plant-developed-and-built-by-linde-engineering) |
| CF Industries, Donaldsonville, Louisiana, US; start-up announced 14 July 2025 | CO2 dehydration and compression for CCS with ExxonMobil | Up to 2 Mt CO2/yr sequestered; ~1.9 Mt/yr of low-carbon ammonia expected; claims Section 45Q tax credits. On an interim basis, the CO2 is stored via enhanced oil recovery until ExxonMobil's dedicated Rose CCS site is permitted | [CF Industries](https://www.cfindustries.com/newsroom/2025/donaldsonvilleccs) |

Scale check: the Herøya plant's 20,500 t/yr is roughly 1% of Donaldsonville's expected ~1.9 Mt/yr of low-carbon ammonia. This is consistent with the IEA's 2025 cost estimates (a CCS premium of about 30% versus about three times for electrolysis) and its 2050 scenarios, in which electrolysis takes the larger share. Yara also has a binding CO2 transport and storage agreement with Northern Lights, aiming to cut 800,000 t CO2/yr at its Sluiskil ammonia plant. [Yara](https://www.yara.com/corporate-releases/yara-opens-renewable-hydrogen-plant-a-major-milestone/)

## EU Policy Signals for Ammonia Producers and Importers

- **ETS product benchmarks**: free allocation is based on the average emissions of the 10% best-performing EU/EEA installations. Installations above the benchmark get fewer allowances than they need. [European Commission, Allocation to industrial installations](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/areas-action/carbon-markets/eu-emissions-trading-system-eu-ets/free-allocation/allocation-industrial-installations_en) The benchmarks for the ammonia chain have been revised as follows:

| Benchmark | 2021–2025 (allowances/t) | 2026–2030 (allowances/t) | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ammonia | 1.570 | 1.522 | −3.1% |
| Nitric acid | 0.230 | 0.151 | −34% |

Sources: [Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/447, Annex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32021R0447); [Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1412 of 26 June 2026, Annex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202601412). The percentage changes are our arithmetic. Under the 2026/1412 Annex, the 2021–2022 average of the 10% most efficient ammonia installations was 1.630 t CO2e/t. Its recital 8 notes that Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/873 included hydrogen produced from water electrolysis in the hydrogen or ammonia benchmark.
- **CBAM**: fertilisers are a CBAM sector. Under current law, free allocation for CBAM goods is phased out between 2026 and 2034 while importers pay a rising share of the carbon price. See [EU CBAM: Definitive Period from 2026](../policies/eu-cbam-definitive-period-2026.md).
- **Proposed slowdown (not yet law)**: on 17 July 2026 the Commission proposed extending the free-allocation phase-out for CBAM sectors to 2038. [European Commission press release IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf) Under the proposal, 15% of the phased-out free allocation would be reintroduced from 2028. From 2031, all free allocation would depend on a verified decarbonisation investment plan, with 80% released on plan approval and 20% on demonstrated emission reductions. EU leaders aim to agree the reform by the end of Q1 2027. [ICAP, EU Commission publishes EU ETS review proposal (updated 23 Jul 2026)](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal) See also [EU Carbon Market Reform](../policies/eu-carbon-market-reform-2026.md).
- **Renewable hydrogen mandate**: the Renewable Energy Directive requires renewable fuels of non-biological origin (RFNBOs) to make up at least 42% of hydrogen used in industry by 2030 and 60% by 2035. RFNBOs must achieve at least 70% GHG savings. [European Commission, Renewable hydrogen](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/topics/eus-energy-system/hydrogen/renewable-hydrogen_en)

## Practical Takeaways

- **Start with the CO2 you already separate.** Ammonia plants already produce a concentrated CO2 stream, and in the IEA's 2025 estimate CCS is the cheaper of the two main near-zero routes. Donaldsonville shows Mt-scale delivery. Buyers should still check whether the CO2 goes to dedicated storage or to enhanced oil recovery.
- **Price the benchmark gap, not just the carbon price.** In the EU, every tonne above the 1.522 allowances/t ammonia benchmark must be covered at the ETS price, and the CBAM factor cuts free allocation further each year. Plant-level verified emissions data therefore has direct financial value for both producers and exporters to the EU.
- **Treat electrolysis as a long-lead option, not a 2030 volume answer.** Yara's Herøya plant, Europe's largest of its kind when it opened in June 2024, supplies hydrogen for only about 20 kt NH3 a year, and the 2025 cost premium is about three times that of conventional production. Mandates (RED RFNBO quotas), auctions (as in India) and offtake contracts are what close the gap.
- **Don't ignore the field.** Most of a fertiliser's life-cycle footprint comes after the factory gate. Product claims and procurement policies that cover only production emissions miss most of the total.
