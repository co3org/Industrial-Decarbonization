# Carbon Capture, Utilization, and Storage (CCUS) for Steel and Cement Decarbonization

## Overview
Carbon capture is essential for hard-to-abate process emissions in steel (from blast furnaces) and cement (from calcination). Technologies include post-combustion capture, oxy-fuel, and emerging direct air or electrochemical methods. The IEA and others project CCUS could abate 20-30% of industrial emissions by 2050 in these sectors.

## Steel Sector Applications
Blast furnace gas (BFG) capture targets CO2-rich off-gases. Recent advances include solvent-based and membrane systems.

### Shougang Group and Rio Tinto Industrial-Scale Trial (2026)
In September 2026, Shougang Group and Rio Tinto commissioned an industrial-scale carbon capture trial facility for blast furnace gas at Shougang’s Jingtang base in China. The facility processes up to 3,000 cubic meters of BFG per hour and captures up to 10,000 tonnes of CO2 annually. This builds on a 2024 pilot and supports their 2022 MoU on low-carbon ironmaking technologies, including sintering optimization and CCUS. [Source](https://www.riotinto.com/en/news/releases/2026/shougang-group-and-rio-tinto-commission-an-industrial-scale-carbon-capture-trial-facility-for-blast-furnace-gas)

This demonstrates scalable CCUS retrofits for existing BF-BOF operations, a critical bridge while hydrogen DRI scales.

## Cement Sector Applications
Cement kilns use amine scrubbing or calcium looping. Projects like Norcem Brevik highlight deployment.

## Challenges and Enablers
- High energy penalties and costs.
- Infrastructure for transport/storage.
- Policy support via 45Q, EU ETS, and innovation funds.
