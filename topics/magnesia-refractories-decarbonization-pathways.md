# Magnesia and Refractories Decarbonization Pathways (Europe, 2026)

Magnesia (magnesium oxide, MgO) is the preferred raw material for the heat-resistant linings, called refractories, of the furnaces and kilns that make steel, cement, lime, glass and non-ferrous metals. Its melting point of about 2,800 °C is why it is used there. [JRC, CLM BREF (2013), p. 297](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/CLM_Published_def_0.pdf) Most of the world's magnesia (5.8 of 6.4 million tonnes in 2003) is made by heating mined magnesite (magnesium carbonate, MgCO3) in kilns. [JRC, CLM BREF (2013), p. 296](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/CLM_Published_def_0.pdf) This releases CO2 from the rock itself, on top of the CO2 from the fuel, so the sector has the same core problem as cement and lime.

This page draws on the EU reference document for the sector (the JRC's Best Available Techniques Reference Document, or BREF, for the Production of Cement, Lime and Magnesium Oxide, adopted in 2013), USGS mineral statistics, EU legislation, a 2026 peer-reviewed pilot study and company reports. The BREF figures describe plants in the mid-2000s and are the most recent official EU ranges.

## Sector Profile

- **Mining**: the USGS estimates world magnesite mine production at about 21 million tonnes in 2025, of which China mined about 12.7 Mt. EU producers mined about 1.75 Mt (Austria 650 kt, Spain 640 kt, Slovakia 330 kt, Greece 130 kt), roughly 8% of the world total. Brazil (1.8 Mt), Russia (1.7 Mt) and Turkey (1.6 Mt) are the other large producers. [USGS, Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026: Magnesium Compounds](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-magnesium-compounds.pdf)
- **Producers**: the BREF counted nine producers of sintered magnesia with 14 plants in the EU-27, in Greece, Spain, Austria, Poland and Slovakia. [JRC, CLM BREF (2013), pp. 296–297](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/CLM_Published_def_0.pdf) Europe's share of world output has fallen over time: European countries made 32% of world magnesia in 1981, and the EU later imposed anti-dumping duties on magnesia imports. [JRC, CLM BREF (2013), pp. 295–296](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/CLM_Published_def_0.pdf)
- **Products and uses**: dead-burned (sintered) magnesia goes mainly into refractories; caustic calcined magnesia is used in feed and fertilizer, steel slag conditioning, construction, paper, chemicals and environmental protection; fused magnesia, made in electric arc furnaces, is produced in Europe only in very small quantities. [JRC, CLM BREF (2013), pp. 298–299](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/CLM_Published_def_0.pdf) Of world magnesia in 2003, 65% went into refractories for steel, 15% into refractories for cement, 7% into other refractories (such as for glass and non-ferrous metals) and 13% into other uses. [JRC, CLM BREF (2013), p. 299](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/CLM_Published_def_0.pdf)
- **Imports**: China is the leading producer and the main source of magnesia and magnesite imports into the United States. [USGS, Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-magnesium-compounds.pdf)

## Emissions and Energy Baseline

All ranges below are from the 2013 BREF, per tonne of magnesia made by the dry process route from magnesite.

| Process CO2 (from the rock) | 1.092 t/t in theory; around 1 t/t in practice | [BREF, pp. 312, 314](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/CLM_Published_def_0.pdf) |
| Fuel CO2 (sintered magnesia) | 0.4–1.3 t/t | [BREF, p. 314](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/CLM_Published_def_0.pdf) |
| Fuel energy | 6–12 GJ/t | [BREF, p. 313](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/CLM_Published_def_0.pdf) |
| Electricity (sintered or caustic magnesia) | 75–180 kWh/t | [BREF, p. 313](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/CLM_Published_def_0.pdf) |
| Electricity (fused magnesia, arc furnace) | 3,500–4,500 kWh/t, and double or more for very pure grades | [BREF, p. 313](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/CLM_Published_def_0.pdf) |
| Firing temperature | calcination starts at 550–800 °C; sintering at 1,600–2,200 °C | [BREF, p. 302](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/CLM_Published_def_0.pdf) |

- **Fuels**: natural gas and petroleum coke were the main kiln fuels, with fuel oil also used. [JRC, CLM BREF (2013), pp. 302, 312](https://bureau-industrial-transformation.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2019-11/CLM_Published_def_0.pdf)
- **What dominates**: process CO2 from the rock is fixed by chemistry and cannot be cut by changing fuel. RHI Magnesita, the global market leader in refractories, [MCi Carbon, Myrtle](https://mcicarbon.com/myrtle/) put process emissions at 46% of its Scope 1 emissions at an industry forum in 2024. [IMFORMED, MagForum 2024 report](https://imformed.com/magnesia-supply-demand-insights/)
- **Refractory footprint**: RHI Magnesita states that making one tonne of refractories typically emits 1.7 t of CO2 across Scopes 1, 2 and 3, mostly from raw material production, and that its total emissions fell from 6.2 Mt in the past to 4.6 Mt in 2022 through more recycling and better energy use. [RHI Magnesita, Towards a clean industrial deal (Oct 2024)](https://www.rhimagnesita.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/final-rhi-white-paper-a4-web.pdf)

## Decarbonization Levers

### 1. Recycling used refractories

Recycled refractory material reduces the need for newly fired magnesia. RHI Magnesita reports that it exceeded its global recycling target of 15% in 2025 and reached more than 22% on average across Europe. It says it has cut its CO2 intensity (Scope 1, 2 and Scope 3 raw materials) by 15% since 2018. [RHI Magnesita, Full-year results 2025 (2 Mar 2026)](https://ir.rhimagnesita.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/rhim-full-year-results-2025.pdf)

### 2. Fuel switching

- **Biomass**: Grecian Magnesite reported in 2024 that biomass supplied 25% of its kiln energy overall (near 80% for caustic calcined magnesia and 15% or more for dead-burned magnesia, where petroleum coke remains the main fuel). [IMFORMED, MagForum 2024 report](https://imformed.com/magnesia-supply-demand-insights/)
- **Hydrogen**: RHI Magnesita sees low-carbon and green hydrogen as a viable replacement for fossil fuels because sintering needs temperatures that direct electric heating cannot reach, but says remote plants outside industrial clusters struggle to get hydrogen supply at the right price. [RHI Magnesita, Towards a clean industrial deal (Oct 2024)](https://www.rhimagnesita.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/final-rhi-white-paper-a4-web.pdf)

### 3. Carbon capture and utilization

Fuel switching does not touch the process CO2, so producers are testing capture.

- **Hochfilzen, Austria (mineral carbonation)**: RHI Magnesita and MCi Carbon plan the first carbon capture and utilization (CCU) plant in the refractory industry at Hochfilzen. It is designed to capture and mineralize 50,000 t of CO2 a year into mineral products, with operation planned for 2028. [MCi Carbon, Myrtle](https://mcicarbon.com/myrtle/) The CCUpScale research project (RHI Magnesita, MCi Carbon, AIT and the University of Technology Sydney) received over €3.8 million from the Austrian Climate and Energy Fund under the Australia-Austria industrial decarbonization program. [Green Tech Austria, 16 Jan 2025](https://www.greentech.at/en/rhi-magnesita-and-mci-carbon-take-a-step-closer-to-establishing-the-worlds-first-ccu-plant-in-the-refractory-industry-in-austria/) [AIT, CCUpScale](https://www.ait.ac.at/en/research-topics/efficiency-in-industrial-processes-systems/projects/ccupscale) RHI Magnesita then secured a €30 million Austrian government grant for the first industrial-scale plant. [RHI Magnesita, Full-year results 2025](https://ir.rhimagnesita.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/rhim-full-year-results-2025.pdf) The company estimated the plant's capital cost at around €100 million. [RHI Magnesita, Towards a clean industrial deal (Oct 2024)](https://www.rhimagnesita.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/final-rhi-white-paper-a4-web.pdf) MCi Carbon opened its Myrtle demonstration plant in Newcastle, Australia, on 17 June 2026, where it is running a validation campaign with RHI Magnesita toward a commercial plant targeted for 2030. [MCi Carbon, 17 Jun 2026](https://mcicarbon.com/myrtle-opening-worlds-first-carbon-refinery/)
- **Yerakini, Greece (electrochemical capture)**: the ConsenCUS project, funded by the EU's Horizon 2020 program, tested a 500 Nm3/h pilot on rotary kiln flue gas at Grecian Magnesite's calcination plant in Chalkidiki from 23 January to 6 June 2025. It captured up to 9.4 kg of CO2 an hour using electricity instead of heat; power use averaged 21.77 GJ per tonne of CO2 and fell to 9.17 GJ/t in the best configurations. Captured CO2 was converted to formic acid (formate). [Borgquist et al., Separation and Purification Technology 394 (2026) 137401](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.seppur.2026.137401) [ConsenCUS, 13 Mar 2025](https://consencus.eu/news/consencus-third-and-final-demo-site-in-full-swing/)

## Policy Context

- **EU ETS**: calcination of magnesite in rotary kilns or other furnaces with a capacity above 50 tonnes a day is an EU ETS activity. [Directive 2003/87/EC, Annex I (consolidated)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02003L0087-20240301) See [EU ETS Free-Allocation Benchmarks 2026-2030](../policies/eu-ets-benchmarks-2026-2030.md) and [EU Carbon Market Reform 2026](../policies/eu-carbon-market-reform-2026.md).
- **CBAM**: magnesia and refractories are **not** CBAM goods. CBAM currently applies to cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen. [European Commission, CBAM](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en) RHI Magnesita warns that rising carbon prices and the gradual phase-out of free allowances for process emissions raise its costs and the risk of carbon leakage. [RHI Magnesita, Towards a clean industrial deal (Oct 2024)](https://www.rhimagnesita.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/final-rhi-white-paper-a4-web.pdf) See [EU CBAM Definitive Period from 2026](../policies/eu-cbam-definitive-period-2026.md).

## Practical Takeaways

- **Process CO2 is the core problem.** About 1 t of CO2 per tonne of magnesia comes from the rock, so fuel switching alone cannot reach net zero.
- **Recycling is the lever working today.** It cuts the amount of new magnesia that has to be fired, and the market leader already recycles more than 20% in Europe.
- **Capture is at pilot stage.** The two European projects on this page are a 50,000 t/yr mineralization plant planned for 2028 and an electrochemical pilot that captured kilograms per hour.
- **Steel buyers carry part of this footprint.** Refractories are a Scope 3 input for steel, cement and glass makers, so their low-carbon procurement can pull these changes forward.

## Open Questions

- Will Hochfilzen start on time in 2028, and will mineral products made from captured CO2 be recognized as carbon-free in EU rules?
- Can electrochemical capture bring its energy use down enough to compete with heat-based capture?
- How far can refractory recycling rise beyond today's 15–24%?
- Should magnesia be considered for CBAM, given import competition and the planned phase-out of free allocation?
- Will the BREF data (2013) be updated to reflect today's plants?
