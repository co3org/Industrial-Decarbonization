# Nitric and Adipic Acid N2O Abatement (2026)

Making nitric acid (mostly for fertilizer) and adipic acid (for nylon) creates nitrous oxide (N2O) as an unwanted by-product. These two processes are the largest industrial sources of N2O. [NACAG, Nitrous oxide emissions from nitric acid production](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/transforming-the-sector/nitrous-oxide-emissions-from-nitric-acid-production/) N2O has a global warming potential 273 times that of CO2 over 100 years, and what is emitted today stays in the atmosphere for more than 100 years on average. [US EPA, Understanding Global Warming Potentials](https://www.epa.gov/ghgemissions/understanding-global-warming-potentials)

Unlike most of the sectors in this repository, the main fix here is not a new production route. The gas can be destroyed with catalysts that are already on the market and, by heavy-industry standards, cheap. The gap is about who has installed them, not about technology.

This page draws on the IPCC inventory guidelines, the EU reference document on best available techniques (BREF) for the sector, EU legislation, and the Nitric Acid Climate Action Group (NACAG), an initiative launched by the German government. [NACAG, MOP38 side event (17 Nov 2026)](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/nacag-co-hosts-mop38-side-event-why-nitrous-oxide-belongs-under-the-montreal-protocol/)

## Where the N2O Comes From

- **Nitric acid**: ammonia is reacted with air over a platinum-rhodium-palladium gauze to make NO, which is then turned into nitric acid. A side reaction in the same step forms N2O. [NACAG, Nitrous oxide emissions from nitric acid production](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/transforming-the-sector/nitrous-oxide-emissions-from-nitric-acid-production/) How much N2O forms depends on things like pressure, temperature, catalyst make-up and age, and burner design. [IPCC 2006 Guidelines, Vol. 3, Ch. 3, Section 3.3.1](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2006gl/pdf/3_Volume3/V3_3_Ch3_Chemical_Industry.pdf)
- **Uses**: around 75–80% of nitric acid goes into fertilizers, mainly ammonium nitrate. It is also used for explosives in mining and construction. [NACAG](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/transforming-the-sector/nitrous-oxide-emissions-from-nitric-acid-production/)
- **Adipic acid and caprolactam**: these nylon raw materials also release N2O when they are made. [NACAG](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/transforming-the-sector/nitrous-oxide-emissions-from-nitric-acid-production/) Glyoxal manufacture and nuclear fuel reprocessing are other sources. [IPCC 2006 Guidelines, Vol. 3, Ch. 3, Section 3.3.1](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2006gl/pdf/3_Volume3/V3_3_Ch3_Chemical_Industry.pdf)

## Emission Factors

IPCC default N2O emission factors for nitric acid, per tonne of 100% nitric acid. These defaults already include the effect of abatement where it is installed. [IPCC 2006 Guidelines, Vol. 3, Ch. 3, Table 3.3, p. 3.23](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2006gl/pdf/3_Volume3/V3_3_Ch3_Chemical_Industry.pdf)

| Plant type | kg N2O per t nitric acid |
| --- | --- |
| High-pressure plant | 9 (±40%) |
| Medium-pressure combustion plant | 7 (±20%) |
| Atmospheric-pressure (low-pressure) plant | 5 (±10%) |
| Plant with process-integrated or tail-gas N2O destruction | 2.5 (±10%) |
| Plant with non-selective catalytic reduction (NSCR) | 2 (±10%) |

NACAG puts a typical nitric acid plant at 6–9 kg N2O per tonne, with 300–1,500 ppm N2O at the absorber outlet. [NACAG](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/transforming-the-sector/nitrous-oxide-emissions-from-nitric-acid-production/)

Adipic acid emits far more per tonne. Uncontrolled, the IPCC default is 300 kg N2O per tonne of adipic acid (±10%). [IPCC 2006 Guidelines, Vol. 3, Ch. 3, Table 3.4, p. 3.30](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2006gl/pdf/3_Volume3/V3_3_Ch3_Chemical_Industry.pdf)

## Abatement Technologies

### Nitric Acid

| Option | Where it sits | Typical N2O cut | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Optimised oxidation reactor and high-efficiency primary catalysts | Up to 40% | [NACAG](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/transforming-the-sector/nitrous-oxide-emissions-from-nitric-acid-production/) |
| Secondary | Catalyst placed just under the primary gauzes in the ammonia oxidation reactor; needs no extra heat or energy | Up to about 95% | [NACAG](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/transforming-the-sector/nitrous-oxide-emissions-from-nitric-acid-production/) |
| Tertiary | Separate catalytic or thermal reactor that treats the tail gas after the absorption column, often combined with NOx removal | Typically more than 95% | [NACAG](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/transforming-the-sector/nitrous-oxide-emissions-from-nitric-acid-production/) |

- **Cost**: NACAG puts technical abatement costs at about €0.50 to €5.00 per tonne of CO2e, depending on the technology and the plant. [NACAG](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/transforming-the-sector/nitrous-oxide-emissions-from-nitric-acid-production/) Its funded projects are expected to cut emissions at about EUR 2 per tonne. Up-front investment can still reach several million euros per plant. [NACAG, MOP38 side event](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/nacag-co-hosts-mop38-side-event-why-nitrous-oxide-belongs-under-the-montreal-protocol/)
- **Performance in 2026**: at the September 2026 ANNA conference, NACAG reported that catalyst efficiency and lifetime keep improving, and that modern catalysts can cut N2O by more than 99%. [NACAG, ANNA Conference 2026](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/successful-exchange-at-the-anna-conference-2026-in-bologna/) NACAG also cites catalysts that last over 15 years. [NACAG, MOP38 side event](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/nacag-co-hosts-mop38-side-event-why-nitrous-oxide-belongs-under-the-montreal-protocol/)

### Adipic Acid

IPCC default destruction factors, which are then multiplied by a utilisation factor that reflects how much of the time the system actually runs. [IPCC 2006 Guidelines, Vol. 3, Ch. 3, Table 3.4, p. 3.30](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2006gl/pdf/3_Volume3/V3_3_Ch3_Chemical_Industry.pdf)

| Technology | Destruction factor | Utilisation factor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Catalytic destruction | 92.5% | 89% |
| Thermal destruction | 98.5% | 97% |
| Recycle to nitric acid | 98.5% | 94% |
| Recycle to adipic acid feedstock | 94% | 89% |

The IPCC notes that utilisation tends to be at the low end in the first 1–5 years of operation, while operators learn the system, and rises after that. [IPCC 2006 Guidelines, Table 3.4, note c](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2006gl/pdf/3_Volume3/V3_3_Ch3_Chemical_Industry.pdf)

## EU Policy

- **Emissions trading**: the EU ETS covers both CO2 and N2O from the production of nitric acid, adipic acid, and glyoxal and glyoxylic acid. [Directive 2003/87/EC, Annex I (consolidated)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02003L0087-20240301) Caprolactam is not listed there.
- **Free-allocation benchmarks 2026–2030**: nitric acid falls from 0.230 to **0.151** allowances per tonne and adipic acid from 2.12 to **1.40**. Both hit the 50% ceiling on cuts from their starting points. The most efficient 10% of installations emitted only 0.015 t CO2e per tonne of nitric acid and 0.34 t per tonne of adipic acid in 2021–22, far below the new values. So well-abated EU plants already sit far under the benchmark. [Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1412, Annex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1412/oj/eng); see [EU ETS Free-Allocation Benchmarks 2026-2030](../policies/eu-ets-benchmarks-2026-2030.md)
- **CBAM**: nitric acid (CN 2808 00 00) and potassium nitrate (CN 2834 21 00) are CBAM goods, and their N2O is counted along with their CO2. Ammonia (CN 2814) is covered for CO2 only. [Regulation (EU) 2023/956, Annex I](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R0956) So importers of non-EU nitric acid and nitrate fertilizers pay CBAM on the N2O embedded in them, not just the CO2. See [EU CBAM Definitive Period from 2026](../policies/eu-cbam-definitive-period-2026.md).
- **Best available techniques**: the 2007 BREF sets N2O levels linked to BAT for medium- and high-pressure nitric acid plants. They are 0.12–0.6 kg N2O/t (20–100 ppmv) for new plants and 0.12–1.85 kg N2O/t (20–300 ppmv) for existing plants, averaged over a catalyst campaign. No conclusion was drawn for low/medium-pressure plants. Industry and one Member State disagreed for existing plants and argued that the range should include 2.5 kg N2O/t. [JRC, LVIC-AAF BREF (Aug 2007), Executive Summary, Table III, p. vi](https://eippcb.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2022-03/LVIC-AAF.pdf)

## Global Status (2026)

- **Uptake**: of the roughly 500 nitric acid plants worldwide, around 100 have installed N2O abatement. Only about half of the estimated 21 adipic acid plants abate N2O. [NACAG, MOP38 side event (17 Nov 2026)](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/nacag-co-hosts-mop38-side-event-why-nitrous-oxide-belongs-under-the-montreal-protocol/)
- **Policy gap**: only 11 countries, with about 13% of global N2O emissions, have N2O reduction targets in their Nationally Determined Contributions. N2O is now the most significant ozone-depleting emission but is not controlled under the Montreal Protocol. At MOP38 in Kigali (November 2026), NACAG and the Environmental Investigation Agency held a side event on bringing it under the Protocol. Options discussed included asking the Technology and Economic Assessment Panel for a report on the feasibility, costs and benefits of industrial N2O abatement. [NACAG, MOP38 side event](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/nacag-co-hosts-mop38-side-event-why-nitrous-oxide-belongs-under-the-montreal-protocol/)
- **NACAG pipeline**: funded projects are expected to cut up to 2.6 Mt CO2e per year directly, and 21.9 Mt CO2e in total by 2040. Eleven countries have committed to long-term low-carbon nitric acid production. [NACAG, MOP38 side event](https://www.nitricacidaction.org/nacag-co-hosts-mop38-side-event-why-nitrous-oxide-belongs-under-the-montreal-protocol/)

## Key Takeaways

- Abatement at nitric and adipic acid plants is one of the cheapest large cuts in heavy industry: commercial catalysts remove 95–99% of N2O, at a few euros per tonne of CO2e.
- What drives uptake is regulation plus a price signal, not technology. Under the EU ETS, the most efficient tenth of EU nitric acid plants averages about a tenth of the 2026–2030 benchmark. Worldwide, only about one nitric acid plant in five abates.
- For buyers of fertilizer, explosives and nylon: at 6–9 kg N2O per tonne, unabated nitric acid carries roughly 1.6–2.5 t CO2e per tonne from N2O alone (our calculation, using a GWP of 273). Ask suppliers for measured (continuously monitored) N2O data, not default factors.
