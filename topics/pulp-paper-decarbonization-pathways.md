# Pulp and Paper Decarbonization Pathways (Europe, 2026)

The pulp and paper industry is an energy-intensive sector with an unusual starting point: most of its fuel is already biomass, much of it process residues, and almost all the electricity it generates on site comes from combined heat and power (CHP). [Cepi Key Statistics 2025, p. 29](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Cepi-Key-Statistics-2025-FINAL.pdf) Its remaining fossil emissions come mainly from natural gas. [Cepi 2050 Decarbonisation Outlook (2026), p. 3](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2050-Decarbonisation-outlook.pdf)

This page uses data from the Confederation of European Paper Industries (Cepi), the industry's European trade body. Its figures cover Cepi member countries, not the whole world. For the JRC's short summary of emerging technologies in this sector, see [EU JRC Factsheets on Emerging Technologies for Hard-to-Abate Industries (2026)](eu-emerging-technologies-hard-to-abate-industries-2026.md).

- **Fuel mix (2024)**: biomass 66.0% of fuel consumption, gas 29.2%, coal 1.3%, fuel oil 1.2%, other fossil fuels 0.6%, other fuels 1.7%. Total fuel use was about 1.06 million TJ. [Cepi Key Statistics 2025, p. 29](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Cepi-Key-Statistics-2025-FINAL.pdf)
- **Electricity (2024)**: total consumption 81,656 GWh, of which 43,328 GWh was generated on site. 95.7% of on-site generation came from CHP. [Cepi Key Statistics 2025, p. 29](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Cepi-Key-Statistics-2025-FINAL.pdf)
- **Efficiency trend**: specific primary energy consumption fell from 15.78 TJ/kt in 1991 to 13.29 TJ/kt in 2024 (−15.8%), and specific electricity use fell from 1.20 to 0.90 GWh/kt (−24.8%). [Cepi Key Statistics 2025, p. 30](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Cepi-Key-Statistics-2025-FINAL.pdf)
- **Recycling**: the European paper recycling rate was 76.4% in both 2024 and 2025. Use of paper for recycling equalled 58.5% of paper and board production in 2025 (Cepi's utilisation rate). [Cepi Key Statistics 2025, pp. 5 and 26](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Cepi-Key-Statistics-2025-FINAL.pdf)
- **Shrinking output, falling utilisation**: in 2025 paper and board mills ran at 80.7% of capacity, down from 84.9% in 2020. [Cepi Key Statistics 2025, p. 5](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Cepi-Key-Statistics-2025-FINAL.pdf) Cepi attributes past emission cuts to three things: cleaner energy (mainly replacing fossil fuels with biomass), modernisation of older assets, and structural change. Paper and board capacity has fallen by about 9 Mt since 2011, partly through closures of older graphic paper mills. [Cepi 2050 Decarbonisation Outlook, p. 2](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2050-Decarbonisation-outlook.pdf)

## Where the Remaining Emissions Are

Cepi's 2026 outlook splits current emissions by mill type. Stand-alone mills, and recycled and virgin-fibre mills that rely on natural gas, make up over 55% of capacity and almost 75% of GHG emissions. They therefore offer the largest remaining reduction opportunity. [Cepi 2050 Decarbonisation Outlook, p. 3](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2050-Decarbonisation-outlook.pdf)

| Mill type | Emissions (Mt CO2e) | Capacity (Mt) | Main lever named by Cepi |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stand-alone recycling | 9.2 | 45 | Electrification; growing adoption of mechanical vapour recompression (MVR) and heat pumps |
| Stand-alone virgin-fibre | 4.9 | 15 | Less reliance on natural gas: replacing ageing fuel-fired assets, fuel switching, more electrification |
| Integrated chemical (kraft) | 3.3 | 28 | Unlocking value from biogenic carbon and CO2 streams |
| Integrated mechanical | 1.3 | 10 | Lower energy intensity through capacity modernisation and electrification |
| Market pulp | 0.6 | 10 | Carbon negativity through capture and storage of biogenic CO2 |

Source: [Cepi 2050 Decarbonisation Outlook, p. 3](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2050-Decarbonisation-outlook.pdf). The figures are read from the outlook's chart.

## Decarbonization Pathways

Cepi's updated 2026 outlook, based on a study by Vision Hunters, concludes that the European industry can reach net-zero emissions by 2050. It projects cumulative emission reductions of 19.2 Mt CO2e between 2025 and 2050, against the 14.0 Mt CO2e expected in the original 2011 roadmap (revised in 2017). [Cepi 2050 Decarbonisation Outlook, pp. 1–2](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2050-Decarbonisation-outlook.pdf) The five levers in the outlook are:

1. **Capacity modernisation**: replacing old, less efficient production assets.
2. **Process optimisation and energy efficiency**.
3. **Fuel switching** to biomass and other fossil-free fuels and heat.
4. **Electrification** of industrial processes. Fuel switching delivered most of the cuts over the last decade. The outlook expects electrification to become the dominant lever after 2030, as more fossil-free electricity becomes available and new technologies mature.
5. **Breakthrough technologies**: CCUS and bio-CCUS, advanced drying systems, hydrogen applications and other emerging solutions. Many of the most important ones are not expected to be deployed at scale until after 2030.

Source for all five: [Cepi 2050 Decarbonisation Outlook, pp. 2–4](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2050-Decarbonisation-outlook.pdf). The cumulative milestones in the outlook are −3.1 Mt CO2e by 2030, −5.8 by 2035, −9.7 by 2040, −13.8 by 2045 and −19.2 by 2050.

### Biogenic CO2: From Emission Source to Removal Opportunity

Kraft (chemical) pulp mills burn large amounts of biomass, so their CO2 is mostly biogenic. Cepi estimates that integrated chemical and market pulp mills could provide an extra 2.5–10 Mt CO2e a year of carbon removal potential in 2050 by capturing and storing this biogenic CO2. [Cepi 2050 Decarbonisation Outlook, p. 3](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2050-Decarbonisation-outlook.pdf) The upper end of that range is half of the sector's 20.0 Mt of direct fossil CO2 in 2025 (our arithmetic, using [Cepi Key Statistics 2025](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Cepi-Key-Statistics-2025-FINAL.pdf)). Storing it depends on CO2 transport and storage infrastructure, which Cepi lists as a critical external enabler. [Cepi 2050 Decarbonisation Outlook, p. 4](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2050-Decarbonisation-outlook.pdf)

### Investment and Cost

- **Cumulative investment need**: about €5 billion by 2030, €50 billion by 2040 and €80 billion by 2050, with annual investment peaking between 2030 and 2040. [Cepi 2050 Decarbonisation Outlook, p. 5](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2050-Decarbonisation-outlook.pdf)
- **Rising cost per lever**: the outlook gives indicative figures of about €100/MWh for process improvement, about €250–400/MWh for fuel switching and for electrification, and about €1,000/MWh for breakthrough technology. The outlook does not define these figures further. They show the move from mature, cost-effective measures to more capital-intensive technologies with less certain payback. [Cepi 2050 Decarbonisation Outlook, p. 5](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2050-Decarbonisation-outlook.pdf)

### External Enablers Cepi Says Are Critical

- Availability of sustainable biomass and fossil-free energy, both heat and electricity
- Expansion of electricity grids and of CO2 transport and storage infrastructure
- Supportive climate, energy and carbon market policies
- Commercialisation and scale-up of breakthrough technologies

Source: [Cepi 2050 Decarbonisation Outlook, p. 4](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2050-Decarbonisation-outlook.pdf). The JRC's February 2026 factsheet, *Mapping the transition of the EU pulp and paper industry to carbon neutrality*, gives an EU-level view of emission sources, technology-specific CO2 abatement costs and technology maturity from 2025 to 2050. [JRC Publications Repository, JRC144089](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC144089)

## Policy Context

- Paper and pulp are **not** CBAM goods. CBAM currently applies to cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen. [European Commission, CBAM](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en) See [EU CBAM: Definitive Period from 2026](../policies/eu-cbam-definitive-period-2026.md) and, for the EU ETS review proposal, [EU Carbon Market Reform](../policies/eu-carbon-market-reform-2026.md).

## Practical Takeaways

- **The fossil gas is in stand-alone mills.** Nearly half of the sector's remaining emissions (9.2 of the roughly 19 Mt CO2e in Cepi's chart) come from stand-alone recycled-fibre mills. Their steam demand makes heat pumps, MVR and electric boilers the main near-term question. See [Industrial Electrification and Process Heat Decarbonization](industrial-electrification-process-heat-decarbonization.md) and [High-Temperature Thermal Energy Storage](high-temperature-thermal-energy-storage-industrial-decarbonization.md).
- **Electrification moves emissions onto the grid.** In 2024 the sector purchased about 47 TWh of electricity (about 38 TWh net of sales), and Cepi reports 8.65 Mt of indirect CO2 for that year. More electrification lowers total emissions only as fast as the power supply decarbonises. [Cepi Key Statistics 2025, pp. 29–30](https://www.cepi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Cepi-Key-Statistics-2025-FINAL.pdf)
- **Kraft mills are potential removal suppliers, not just emitters.** Bio-CCS at chemical and market pulp mills could deliver 2.5–10 Mt CO2e of removals a year by 2050, if CO2 transport and storage access and a business case for removals exist.
- **Report fossil and biogenic CO2 separately.** With biomass at two-thirds of fuel use, a single "CO2 per tonne" figure can hide very different fossil footprints and removal potentials between mill types.
