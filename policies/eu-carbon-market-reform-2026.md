# EU Carbon Market Reform: ETS Review Proposal of 17 July 2026

On 17 July 2026 the European Commission tabled a targeted revision of the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) — proposal [COM(2026) 616 final](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en) — to align Phase 5 (2031–2040) with the EU's binding 90% net greenhouse-gas reduction target for 2040, and to turn the carbon market into a larger industrial-investment engine under the Clean Industrial Deal. [European Commission press release IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf); [ICAP, EU Commission publishes EU ETS review proposal (updated 23 Jul 2026)](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal)

**This is a legislative proposal, not yet law.** It follows the ordinary legislative procedure. EU leaders aim to agree the reform by the end of the first quarter of 2027. [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal) Until adopted, current ETS and CBAM rules continue to apply. For the definitive CBAM period that started on 1 January 2026, see [EU CBAM: Definitive Period from 2026](eu-cbam-definitive-period-2026.md).

Since launch in 2005 the EU ETS has cut covered-sector emissions by about 50% and generated more than €270 billion in auction revenues reinvested in innovation, industrial decarbonisation and energy-system modernisation. [IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf)

## What the Proposal Changes for Industry

The elements that matter most for steel, cement, aluminium, fertilisers, chemicals and other energy-intensive industries are:

1. A slower free-allocation phase-out for CBAM sectors (to 2038 instead of 2034), with 15% of phased-out free allocation reintroduced from 2028.
2. Full investment conditionality on free allocation from 2031.
3. A shallower cap trajectory (lower Linear Reduction Factor) for 2031–2040.
4. The Industrial Decarbonisation Bank, with an Investment Booster before 2030.
5. Integration of permanent carbon removals and a limited opening to international credits from 2036.
6. Stronger rules on how Member States spend ETS auction revenues.

## Free Allocation and the CBAM Factor

### Current law (still in force)

Free allocation for CBAM goods is being phased out between 2026 and 2034 under Article 10a(1a) of the ETS Directive. The "CBAM factor" is the share of free allocation retained:

| Year | CBAM factor (current law) | Share phased out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2026 | 97.5% | 2.5% |
| 2027 | 95.0% | 5% |
| 2028 | 90.0% | 10% |
| 2029 | 77.5% | 22.5% |
| 2030 | 51.5% | 48.5% |
| 2031 | 39.0% | 61% |
| 2032 | 26.5% | 73.5% |
| 2033 | 14.0% | 86% |
| 2034 | 0% | 100% |

Source: [European Commission, CBAM Guidance Document 4 on the free allocation adjustment (14 Aug 2026), Table 2-1](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/3aa2c730-4f1b-4524-8f71-d9cfe4880ac7_en?filename=Guidance+No.+4+-+CBAM+calculation+of+the+free+allocation+adjustment+to+the+number+of+CBAM+certificates+to+be+surrendered.pdf). The right-hand column is 100% minus the CBAM factor.

Benchmarks for 2026–2030 were adopted on 26 June 2026 by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1412. Free allocation is calculated from the average emissions of the 10% best-performing EU/EEA installations for each product. [European Commission, Allocation to industrial installations](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/areas-action/carbon-markets/eu-emissions-trading-system-eu-ets/free-allocation/allocation-industrial-installations_en)

### Proposed slowdown (COM(2026) 616 — not yet law)

The Commission proposes that 15% of the free allocation phased out by the CBAM factor be reintroduced from 2028, slowing the CBAM phase-in and extending the free-allocation phase-out until 2038. [IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf); [COM(2026) 616 final, explanatory memorandum](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en); [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal)

The proposed Article 10a(1a) text sets these CBAM factors:

| Year | CBAM factor under COM(2026) 616 |
| --- | --- |
| 2026 | 97.5% (unchanged) |
| 2027 | 95% (unchanged) |
| 2028 | 91.5% |
| 2029 | 81% |
| 2030 | 59% |
| 2031 | 48% |
| 2032 | 37.5% |
| 2033 | 27% |
| 2034–2037 | 15% |
| From 2038 | 0% |

Source: [COM(2026) 616 final](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en), proposed amendment to Article 10a(1a). Recital 58 of the same proposal describes free allocation as phased out fully by the end of 2037, with a 0% CBAM factor from 2038.

Nothing in the proposal changes 2026 or 2027 obligations for EU producers or CBAM declarants. The steep part of today's schedule (2029–2034) is where the proposed flattening has the largest effect.

A separate proposal tabled the same day would revise heat and fuel (fallback) benchmark values for 2026–2030, which the Commission says would increase free allocation to industry by about €6 billion over that period. [IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf) ICAP reports that adjustment as roughly 80 million additional allowances, excluding oil and gas activities. [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal)

## Investment Conditionality from 2031

Under the proposal, free allocation and the carbon-leakage list continue through 2040, with updated benchmark rules for 2031–2035 and 2036–2040 and a larger cross-sectoral correction-factor buffer of 4%. [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal); [COM(2026) 616 final](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en)

From the five-year allocation period starting 1 January 2031, **all** free allocation would depend on a verified decarbonisation investment plan for investments in the EU:

- **80%** of the period's free allocation is released when the plan is approved.
- **20%** is released only after verified emission reductions by the end of that five-year period.
- Operators that relocate relevant activities outside the EU must return allowances received under this provision.

Sources: [COM(2026) 616 final, explanatory memorandum](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en); [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal); [IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf)

## Cap Trajectory (Linear Reduction Factor)

| Period | Linear Reduction Factor |
| --- | --- |
| 2024–2027 (current law) | 4.3% |
| From 2028 (current law) | 4.4% |
| 2031–2035 (proposed) | 3.7% |
| 2036–2040 (proposed) | 1.7% |

Sources: current rates from [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal); proposed rates from [IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf) and the proposed Article 9 text in [COM(2026) 616 final](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en) ("The linear factor shall be 3.7% from 2031 to 2035 and 1.7% from 2036").

The shallower post-2030 path is designed so that new allowances continue to be issued into the 2040s, rather than the supply ending around 2039 if today's rates were simply extended. [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal)

## Industrial Decarbonisation Bank and Revenue Recycling

- **Industrial Decarbonisation Bank (IDB)**: proposed as an EU-level entity from 2028 under new Article 10cc, with planned funding of up to €100 billion for industrial decarbonisation. [IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf); [COM(2026) 616 final](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en) See also [EU Industrial Decarbonisation Bank (IDB) 2026](eu-industrial-decarbonisation-bank-2026.md).
- **Investment Booster (first phase, 2028–2031)**: reserves 400 million allowances for fixed carbon premia on a first-come, first-served basis, with a dedicated share for lower-income Member States, completion deadlines and completion bonds. [COM(2026) 616 final](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en); [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal)
- **From 2031**: competitive Carbon Contracts for Difference or carbon premia. [COM(2026) 616 final](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en)
- **National auction revenues**: Member States would have to allocate at least 50% of ETS revenues to defined priority areas (clean energy and grids, low-carbon transport, industrial decarbonisation including electrification and CCS, waste, research and innovation). Fossil-fuel lock-in spending would be prohibited. Together with the Bank and related instruments, the Commission puts pre-2030 investment support above €100 billion. [IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf); [COM(2026) 616 final, explanatory memorandum](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en)
- **Innovation Fund and Modernisation Fund** continue, with the Modernisation Fund's eligible areas expanded to electrification, industrial decarbonisation and CCS/CCU (fossil generation remains ineligible). [COM(2026) 616 final](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en)

## Removals, International Credits and Market Stability

- **Permanent carbon removals**: proposed new Article 9c would raise the ETS cap by 250 million allowances (2031–2040), auctioned to fund Commission purchases of CRCF-certified BioCCS and DACCS units, plus a 10 million contingency. Operators may offset their own fossil emissions with BioCCS removals they generate, without going below zero. [COM(2026) 616 final](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en); [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal)
- **International credits**: up to 260 million allowances ring-fenced for 2036–2040 to buy high-quality Paris Agreement Article 6 credits (about 2% of 1990 net GHG emissions, within the Climate Law's 5% ceiling). If high-quality credits prove unavailable, the Commission must report by January 2033 and the LRF would revert toward full domestic 90% ambition. [COM(2026) 616 final](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en); [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal); [IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf)
- **Market Stability Reserve**: the July proposal complements the Commission's 1 April 2026 amendment to stop automatic invalidation of allowances held in the MSR above 400 million. From 2028 the intake rate would fall from 24% to 12%, a lower buffer would be added, and from 2029 all MSR reference points would decline 4% a year with the shrinking cap. [IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf); [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal); [COM(2026) 616 final, Annex II](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en)

## Sectoral Scope Extensions

The proposal also:

- Brings non-hazardous municipal waste incineration and co-incineration (capacity above 3 t/hour) into the ETS, with the surrender obligation phased in from 2031 to 100% by 2034.
- Strengthens aviation coverage (regional extension to arrivals from countries within 5,000 km of the EU from 2029; business aviation in scope) while keeping the "stop the clock" approach on full extra-EEA coverage under review against CORSIA.
- Extends maritime coverage to vessels of 400–5,000 GT and adds a Sustainable Maritime Alternative Propulsion revenue-recycling mechanism.

Sources: [IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf); [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-commission-publishes-eu-ets-review-proposal); [COM(2026) 616 final](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en)

The same package included an Electrification Action Plan. About 70% of EU electricity already comes from clean domestic sources, but the electrification rate of energy demand has stayed near 23% for a decade. An indicative 46% electrification target by 2040 will be assessed in the post-2030 Energy Union package; the Commission estimates that reaching it could cut the EU fossil-fuel import bill by about €260 billion a year by 2040. [IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf)

## Implications for Industrial Decarbonization

- **EU producers in CBAM sectors**: under current law free allocation falls sharply after 2028. If COM(2026) 616 is adopted as proposed, that cliff flattens and a 15% residual free allocation lasts through 2037 — but from 2031 every free allowance depends on a verified EU investment plan. Plant-level MRV and credible decarbonisation plans become the gate to free allocation, not only a CBAM cost input for importers.
- **Exporters to the EU**: 2026–2027 CBAM maths are unchanged. From 2028, a higher CBAM factor would mean a larger free-allocation adjustment and fewer certificates to surrender than under today's schedule — if and when the proposal becomes law. Until then, budget against the current-law table above. See [EU CBAM: Definitive Period from 2026](eu-cbam-definitive-period-2026.md).
- **Investment case**: the IDB's 400 million-allowance Booster (2028–2031) and later CCfDs are meant to close the gap where the carbon price alone has not triggered transformative projects in energy-intensive industry. [COM(2026) 616 final, recital 60](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en)
- **Non-CBAM carbon-leakage sectors**: free allocation continues beyond 2030 under the proposal, but the same 80/20 investment conditionality applies from 2031. [COM(2026) 616 final](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/757bd24a-8413-11f1-bf5e-01aa75ed71a1/language-en)

## Practical Takeaways

- Treat COM(2026) 616 as the ceiling scenario for free allocation through 2038, and current law (phase-out by 2034) as the floor, until co-legislators finish the file.
- Build the 2031 investment-plan conditionality into project timelines now: 80% of free allocation will hinge on an approved plan, and 20% on delivered reductions.
- Track the parallel heat/fuel benchmark proposal (€6 billion / ~80 million allowances for 2026–2030) separately from the CBAM-factor rewrite — it is a different legal act tabled the same day.
- Watch Q1 2027 for political agreement; until then, CBAM certificate prices, the 50% quarterly holding rule from February 2027, and the September 2027 first declaration all follow current law.
