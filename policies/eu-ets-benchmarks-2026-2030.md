# EU ETS Free-Allocation Benchmarks 2026-2030: All 54 Values and What Changed

Free allowances in the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) are handed out using greenhouse gas benchmarks: 52 product benchmarks plus two fallback benchmarks for heat and fuel. Each product benchmark is based on the average emissions of the 10% best-performing installations making that product in the EU and EEA-EFTA states. Installations that meet the benchmark receive, in principle, all the allowances they need; those that do not have to cut emissions or buy allowances. [European Commission, Allocation to industrial installations](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/areas-action/carbon-markets/eu-emissions-trading-system-eu-ets/free-allocation/allocation-industrial-installations_en)

The values for the second half of Phase 4 were set by **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1412 of 26 June 2026**, published in the Official Journal on 29 June 2026 and in force from the day after publication. They apply to free allocation for 2026 to 2030. [Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1412, Articles 1-2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1412/oj/eng)

This page lists every value, compares it with 2021-2025 and with the original starting points, and explains which rules drove the changes.

## How the Values Were Calculated

- **Data years**: the 2026-2030 values use installation data for 2021 and 2022, submitted under Article 11 of the ETS Directive. For each benchmark, the average of the 10% most efficient installations is compared with the original starting point in Annex I to Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/331, giving an annual reduction rate over the 14 years from 2007/2008 to 2021/2022. That rate is then extrapolated over 20 years (2007/2008 to 2027/2028). [Reg. 2026/1412, recital 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1412/oj/eng)
- **Floor and ceiling**: the total reduction over the 20 years must be at least 6% and at most 50%. [Reg. 2026/1412, recital 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1412/oj/eng) For comparison, the 2021-2025 values used 2016-2017 data, a 15-year extrapolation, and a 3% to 24% range. [Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/447, recital 3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2021/447/oj/eng)
- **Refinery-linked benchmarks**: aromatics and syngas still follow the refinery benchmark's adjustment percentage. Hydrogen no longer does, because Directive (EU) 2023/959 decoupled hydrogen from refineries. [Reg. 2026/1412, recital 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1412/oj/eng)
- **Dataset checks**: Member States submitted installation lists by 30 September 2024. The Commission ran completeness and consistency checks, asked authorities for corrections where needed, and held technical exchanges with stakeholders. [Reg. 2026/1412, recital 10](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1412/oj/eng)
- **Installations left out of the calculation**: installations excluded from the ETS under Articles 27 and 27a (recital 11); installations newly brought into scope from 1 January 2024 by Directive (EU) 2023/959 (recital 12); sub-installations whose emissions could not be determined precisely, which affected refinery products, hot metal, sintered dolime, ammonia, steam cracking, phenol/acetone and hydrogen (recital 14). Negative efficiencies caused by exporting low-carbon heat were set to zero (recital 15). [Reg. 2026/1412](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1412/oj/eng)
- **Default factors** used in the calculation: 40.5 t CO2e/TJ for heat imports with unknown emission factors and for heat exports, 36.5 t CO2e/TJ for waste-gas imports, and 0.300 t CO2e/MWh for electricity produced inside a sub-installation. In 2021-2025 the heat and waste-gas values were 53.3 and 48.0 t CO2e/TJ. [Reg. 2026/1412, recital 13](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1412/oj/eng); [Reg. 2021/447, recital 6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2021/447/oj/eng)

## Rule Changes That Matter for Low-Carbon Technologies

Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/873 changed several benchmark definitions, and the 2026-2030 values reflect those changes. [Reg. 2026/1412, recitals 7-9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1412/oj/eng)

- **Cement clinker**: the grey and white clinker benchmarks are open to alternative hydraulic binders, so non-clinker binders can receive allocation under them.
- **Iron ore**: the sintered ore benchmark now covers other agglomerated iron ore products, and the Annex lists it as "agglomerated iron ore".
- **Hydrogen and ammonia**: hydrogen made by water electrolysis now falls under the hydrogen or ammonia benchmark.
- **Steel by direct reduction**: steel made by direct reduction now falls under the hot metal benchmark, but DRI installations were left out when calculating the 2026-2030 hot metal value, as Article 10a(2)(e) of the Directive requires.
- **Electric heat**: heat produced from electricity, measurable or not, now counts under the heat and fuel benchmarks.
- **Electricity in section 2 benchmarks**: for benchmarks that previously had fuel/electricity exchangeability rules (refinery products, EAF steel, ammonia, hydrogen and others in section 2 of Annex I to Reg. 2019/331), the top-10% average includes indirect emissions from electricity use, to keep the values comparable with earlier updates. [Reg. 2026/1412, recital 6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1412/oj/eng)
- **Soda ash**: following the new definition of "emissions" in Directive (EU) 2023/959, intermediary CO2 is now monitored and reported, and these emissions are added to the soda ash benchmark after the reduction rate is applied. [Reg. 2026/1412, recital 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1412/oj/eng) For 2025 alone, Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/389 had already raised the soda ash value from 0.753 to 1.168 allowances/t by adding the stoichiometric factor of 0.415 t CO2/t. [Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/389, recital 2 and Annex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/389/oj/eng)

## Reading the Numbers

All figures in this section come from the table above.

- **Many benchmarks hit the 50% ceiling.** Coke, agglomerated iron ore, nitric and adipic acid, styrene, mineral wool, both EAF steel benchmarks, iron casting, every paper grade, most pulp grades, and the heat and fuel benchmarks are all cut by about 50% from their starting points. For these, the best performers' 2021-22 emissions were far below the benchmark (for example, nitric acid at 0.015 against a benchmark of 0.151), so the ceiling, not real performance, set the value. Most of them fall about 34% from 2021-2025, because the ceiling rose from 24% to 50%.
- **Others sit at the 6% floor.** Hot metal (blast furnace iron), aluminium, ammonia and plaster are cut by about 6% in total, and VCM, S-PVC and plasterboard by under 7%. For these products the 2021-22 top-10% average is close to or above the starting point (hot metal 1.360 against 1.328; ammonia 1.630 against 1.619), so the floor set the value. For VCM, S-PVC and plasterboard the 2021-22 top-10% average is above the 2021-2025 value, so their 2026-2030 values are higher than before. Roof tiles, refinery products, aromatics, syngas and hydrogen also rise slightly or moderately.
- **Hydrogen is up 16.7%** to 7.98 allowances/t, because its value no longer follows the refinery adjustment (recital 4) and now reflects its own data, a 9.8% cut from the starting point.
- **Cement clinker and lime**: grey clinker falls 5.3% to 0.656 and lime 4.4% to 0.693. In both cases the 2021-22 top-10% average is close to or above the new value (grey clinker 0.689; lime 0.771). See [CCUS for Cement Decarbonization](../topics/ccus-cement-decarbonization.md) and [Lime Decarbonization Pathways](../topics/lime-decarbonization-pathways.md).
- **Glass and ceramics are mixed**: float glass and coloured container glass changed by less than 2%, while colourless container glass (-16.6%), glass fibre (-24.9%), facing bricks (-21.7%), pavers (-26.0%) and spray-dried powder (-20.7%) were cut sharply.

## Cross-Sectoral Correction Factor: 100% for 2026-2030

Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1862 of 23 July 2026 sets the CSCF at **100% for every year from 2026 to 2030**, so free allocations are not cut across the board. [Decision 2026/1862, Article 1](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2026/1862/oj/eng) The decision's figures:

- **2026 cap on free allocation**: 1,180,706,960 allowances (the adjusted total) × 43% = 507,703,993. Subtracting 35,357,143 under Article 10a(8) and 40,000,000 for the Social Climate Fund leaves a maximum of **432,346,850 free allowances in 2026**. [Decision 2026/1862, recital 6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2026/1862/oj/eng)
- **Later years**: 434,602,358 (2027), 395,980,088 (2028), 357,357,817 (2029) and 318,735,546 (2030). [Decision 2026/1862, recital 7](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2026/1862/oj/eng)
- **Why no cut is needed**: 125,586,948 allowances left unused in 2021-2025, plus 289,226,630 of the 368,419,533 allowances in the 3% reserve under Article 10a(5a), cover the gap. [Decision 2026/1862, recitals 8-9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2026/1862/oj/eng)
- **CBAM link**: allocation removed by the CBAM factor is not counted in the preliminary amounts used for the CSCF, because those allowances go to the Innovation Fund. [Decision 2026/1862, recital 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2026/1862/oj/eng) For the CBAM factor schedule (97.5% in 2026 down to 51.5% in 2030), see [EU CBAM: Definitive Period from 2026](eu-cbam-definitive-period-2026.md).

## Heat and Fuel Benchmark Revision (Provisional Agreement, 12 November 2026)

The 34% cut to the heat and fuel benchmarks drew industry objections. In recital 16 of Reg. 2026/1412, the Commission committed to propose a separate act to raise allocation under the fallback benchmarks without triggering the CSCF. [Reg. 2026/1412, recital 16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1412/oj/eng)

- **Proposal**: COM(2026) 619 was published on 17 July 2026. Parliament adopted its first-reading amendments on 22 October 2026 and sent the file back to committee for talks with the Council. [European Parliament Legislative Observatory, 2026/0211(COD)](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-file?reference=2026/0211(COD)) The Commission put the extra allocation at about EUR 6 billion over 2026-2030. [European Commission, IP/26/1596](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1596/IP_26_1596_EN.pdf)
- **Deal**: on 12 November 2026 the Council and Parliament reached a provisional agreement worth about **121 million allowances**:
  - about 88 million from the Commission proposal;
  - 28 million that had gone unallocated because installations failed ETS conditionalities (energy audits or climate-neutrality plans);
  - 5 million set aside for highly exposed sectors, with any unused allowances going to the Modernisation and Innovation Funds.

  Heat and fuel benchmarks account for about 25% of total free allocation and cover sectors such as chemicals, pulp and paper, food and beverages, ceramics and glass. [Council of the EU press release, 12 November 2026](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/12/emissions-trading-system-council-and-parliament-agree-to-boost-free-carbon-allowances-for-energy-intensive-industries/)
- **Size of the change**: according to the ceramics association Cerame-Unie, the deal applies the lowest achievable reduction rate to the general heat and fuel benchmarks, about -10% instead of -34% compared with 2021-2025. Most ceramic installations (clay blocks, pipes, tiles, refractories, sanitaryware, expanded clay, tableware) are allocated under the fallback benchmarks. [Cerame-Unie](https://cerameunie.eu/topics/climate-energy/emissions-trading-system/eu-ets-benchmarks-review-co-legislators-agree-the-lowest-possible-fallback-benchmark-reduction-and-recognise-sector-specific-needs-for-the-ceramic-tiles-sector-for-2026-2030/)
- **Status**: the deal is not yet law. The Council and Parliament must endorse it and formally adopt it after legal-linguistic revision. The Commission will then adopt implementing rules on calculating the heat and fuel benchmarks. Until then, the 31.2 and 28.1 allowances/TJ values in Reg. 2026/1412 remain the legal values. [Council of the EU, 12 November 2026](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/12/emissions-trading-system-council-and-parliament-agree-to-boost-free-carbon-allowances-for-energy-intensive-industries/)

## Implications for Industrial Decarbonization

- **The benchmark is the price signal.** Because one product gets one benchmark regardless of technology or fuel, an installation that cuts emissions below the benchmark keeps its allocation and needs fewer allowances. [European Commission, Allocation to industrial installations](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/areas-action/carbon-markets/eu-emissions-trading-system-eu-ets/free-allocation/allocation-industrial-installations_en) The rule changes covering electrolytic hydrogen, alternative binders and DRI-based steel (recitals 8-9) mean these low-carbon routes can now receive allocation under the existing benchmarks.
- **At the 6% floor, even the best plants are above the benchmark.** For blast-furnace iron and ammonia, the 2021-22 top-10% averages (1.360 and 1.630) exceed the 2026-2030 benchmarks (1.248 and 1.522), so even the best installations get fewer free allowances than they need. In CBAM sectors, allocation also shrinks with the CBAM factor. See [Steel Decarbonization Pathways](../topics/steel-decarbonization-pathways.md), [Aluminum Decarbonization Pathways](../topics/aluminum-decarbonization-pathways.md) and [Ammonia and Nitrogen Fertilizer Decarbonization Pathways](../topics/ammonia-fertilizer-decarbonization-pathways.md).
- **Watch the follow-up acts.** Two items are still pending: the final heat and fuel values once the 2026/0211(COD) amendment and its implementing rules are adopted, and the 2031-2040 benchmark rules proposed in the 17 July 2026 ETS review. See [EU Carbon Market Reform: ETS Review Proposal of 17 July 2026](eu-carbon-market-reform-2026.md).
