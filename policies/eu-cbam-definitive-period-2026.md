# EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM): Definitive Period from 2026

The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) puts a carbon price on the emissions embedded in selected carbon-intensive goods imported into the EU, at a level equivalent to what EU producers pay under the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS). It was established by Regulation (EU) 2023/956 of 10 May 2023, ran a reporting-only transitional period from October 2023 to December 2025, and entered its definitive (paying) period on 1 January 2026. [European Commission CBAM Q&A, updated 15 Oct 2026](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)

For industrial decarbonization, CBAM matters because it is the counterpart of the phase-out of free ETS allowances for the same sectors: as EU producers of cement, steel, aluminium, fertilisers and hydrogen lose free allocation between 2026 and 2034, importers of those goods pay a growing share of the same carbon price. [European Commission CBAM Q&A](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)

## Scope

- **Sectors covered**: cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen. [European Commission, CBAM](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en)
- **Why these sectors**: they were chosen for their high risk of carbon leakage and high emission intensity, and once fully phased in they will represent more than 50% of the emissions of the sectors covered by the ETS. [European Commission CBAM Q&A, section 1.7](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)
- **50-tonne threshold**: importers bringing in more than 50 tonnes of CBAM goods per year must become authorised CBAM declarants. Below the threshold, no authorisation is needed, except for hydrogen and electricity, whose importers must always be authorised. [European Commission, CBAM definitive regime](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-definitive-regime_en); [CBAM Q&A, section 1.1](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)
- **Effect of the threshold**: the threshold was introduced by the simplification Regulation (EU) 2025/2083 of 8 October 2025. The Commission estimates it removes around 182,000 importers from CBAM's scope and cuts their administrative costs by about EUR 1,123 million per year. [CBAM Q&A, section 1.2](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en); [COM(2025) 989, explanatory memorandum, p. 8](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/docs_autres_institutions/commission_europeenne/com/2025/0989/COM_COM%282025%290989_EN.pdf)

## How the Obligation Works

1. **Declare**: by 30 September of the year after import, authorised declarants submit an annual CBAM declaration of the emissions embedded in the goods they imported. The first declaration, covering 2026 imports, is due by 30 September 2027. [CBAM Q&A, section 1.1](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)
2. **Surrender certificates**: by the same date, declarants surrender CBAM certificates matching the declared embedded emissions, reduced by (a) a free-allocation adjustment equal to the free allocation an EU producer would receive and (b) any carbon price effectively paid in the country of production. [CBAM Q&A, sections 1.1 and 1.3](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)
3. **Buy certificates**: sales of CBAM certificates start on 1 February 2027. From 2027, declarants must also hold certificates covering at least 50% of the emissions embedded in their imports since the start of the year at the end of each quarter. [CBAM Q&A, sections 1.1 and 3.1](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)
4. **Price**: the certificate price follows EU ETS auction prices. It is a quarterly average in 2026 and a weekly average from 2027. [European Commission, CBAM definitive regime](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-definitive-regime_en)

## Phase-In Schedule (Mirrors ETS Free-Allocation Phase-Out)

Until free allowances in CBAM sectors are fully phased out in 2034, CBAM applies only to the share of emissions that does not benefit from free ETS allowances. [CBAM Q&A, section 1.4](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en) The pace is set by the "CBAM factor" in Article 10a(1a) of the EU ETS Directive, which scales down free allocation for CBAM goods:

| Year | CBAM factor (share of free allocation kept) | Share of free allocation phased out (CBAM phase-in) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2026 | 97.5% | 2.5% |
| 2027 | 95.0% | 5% |
| 2028 | 90.0% | 10% |
| 2029 | 77.5% | 22.5% |
| 2030 | 51.5% | 48.5% |
| 2031 | 39.0% | 61% |
| 2032 | 26.5% | 73.5% |
| 2033 | 14.0% | 86% |
| 2034 | 0% | 100% |

CBAM factor source: [European Commission, CBAM Guidance Document 4 on the free allocation adjustment (14 Aug 2026), Table 2-1](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/3aa2c730-4f1b-4524-8f71-d9cfe4880ac7_en?filename=Guidance+No.+4+-+CBAM+calculation+of+the+free+allocation+adjustment+to+the+number+of+CBAM+certificates+to+be+surrendered.pdf). The right-hand column is simply 100% minus the CBAM factor.

The free-allocation adjustment per tonne of good equals the CBAM factor × the cross-sectoral correction factor (CSCF) × the relevant CBAM benchmark (Equation 2 of the same guidance). CBAM benchmarks are set per CN code in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2620 and are derived from EU ETS benchmarks. [CBAM Guidance Document 4](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/3aa2c730-4f1b-4524-8f71-d9cfe4880ac7_en?filename=Guidance+No.+4+-+CBAM+calculation+of+the+free+allocation+adjustment+to+the+number+of+CBAM+certificates+to+be+surrendered.pdf); [CBAM Q&A, sections 3.8 and 3.9](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)

**Illustrative example** (our arithmetic, using the Commission's own illustration in Guidance Document 4, Figure 2-1: actual emissions of 1.5 t CO2 per tonne of good, a benchmark of 1.1 t CO2/t, and a CSCF of 1.0):

- 2026: 1.5 − (0.975 × 1.0 × 1.1) = 0.4275 t CO2 per tonne of good. At the Q1 2026 price of €75.36, that is about €32 per tonne of good.
- 2034: no CBAM factor applies, so the full 1.5 t CO2 per tonne of good is priced. At the same €75.36 price, that is about €113 per tonne of good.

The gap between a producer's actual emissions and the benchmark is priced from day one. This is why verified, low actual emissions are worth more to exporters than the default values.

## Default Values vs. Actual Emissions

- Where verified actual emissions are not available, declarants may use country- and year-specific default values (Annex I to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2621), except for electricity. [CBAM Q&A, section 4.25](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)
- Default values carry a mark-up of 10% in 2026, 20% in 2027 and 30% from 2028 onwards, with a lower 1% mark-up for fertilisers. The Commission states that for most declarants it should therefore be more advantageous to use actual values where available. [CBAM Q&A, section 4.25](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)
- Emission factors from life-cycle assessment (LCA) databases are not accepted for calculating embedded emissions. [CBAM Q&A, section 4.24](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)
- **Carbon price paid abroad**: a carbon price effectively paid in a third country (not rebated or compensated, for example through free allowances) can be deducted. The Commission plans an implementing act in 2026 on the evidence needed, and is assessing "default carbon prices" per country. [CBAM Q&A, section 1.5](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)

## Pending Revision: Downstream Products and Anti-Circumvention

- On 17 December 2025 the Commission proposed COM(2025) 989 to extend CBAM to selected steel- and aluminium-intensive downstream products (about 180 additional CN codes) from 1 January 2028, and to add anti-circumvention measures. It also makes the rules for imported electricity more flexible; the Commission's preferred option is an average grid emission factor for the exporting country. [COM(2025) 989](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/docs_autres_institutions/commission_europeenne/com/2025/0989/COM_COM%282025%290989_EN.pdf)
- The Commission projects about EUR 0.58 billion a year in revenue from the extension by 2030 and about EUR 0.69 billion by 2035. It expects around 3,800 to 3,900 additional SMEs to face CBAM obligations, while the 50-tonne threshold excludes more than 90% of importers in the newly covered sectors and keeps over 99% of emissions in scope. [COM(2025) 989, explanatory memorandum, pp. 8–9](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/docs_autres_institutions/commission_europeenne/com/2025/0989/COM_COM%282025%290989_EN.pdf)
- On 15 September 2026 the European Parliament adopted its amendments (464 votes to 50, with 159 abstentions) and referred the file back to committee for interinstitutional negotiations. Its position broadens the downstream list beyond the Commission's, applies country-level default values ex ante to high-risk country–good combinations, and adds scrap-content reporting and monitoring of distance sales. It also removes the option to count Paris Agreement Article 6 credits against CBAM obligations. [European Parliament Legislative Observatory, procedure 2025/0419(COD)](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-document-summary/pdf?id=1914940)
- After that vote the file went into negotiations between Parliament and Council. Until a final regulation is adopted, the downstream extension is a proposal, not law; check the procedure file for the outcome.

## Implications for Industrial Decarbonization

- **Exporters to the EU**: from 2026, verified low actual emissions directly reduce the CBAM cost for their customers, and the default-value mark-ups rise to 30% by 2028. Installation-level monitoring, reporting and verification becomes a competitive asset. [CBAM Q&A, sections 1.6 and 4.25](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)
- **EU producers**: the falling CBAM factor means free allocation for CBAM goods shrinks sharply after 2028 (from 90% in 2028 to 51.5% in 2030). This strengthens the business case for low-carbon process routes such as hydrogen-based DRI, CCUS in cement and electrified heat (see the related pages below).
- **Third countries**: because carbon prices effectively paid abroad are deductible, CBAM is designed to encourage producer countries to introduce their own carbon pricing. [CBAM Q&A, sections 1.3 and 1.5](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en)
