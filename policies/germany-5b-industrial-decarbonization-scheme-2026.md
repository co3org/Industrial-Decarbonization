# Germany €6 Billion Industrial Decarbonization Scheme with CCS Expansion (2026)

*Image source: Pexels*

Germany has expanded its Carbon Contracts for Difference (CCfD) program to €6 billion, incorporating carbon capture and storage (CCS) and utilization (CCU) to support energy-intensive industries including chemicals, steel, cement, pulp and paper, ceramics, glass, and gypsum. The initiative, announced in October 2025, aims to bridge the cost gap between low-carbon and conventional production methods through 15-year climate protection contracts awarded via reverse auctions starting in 2026.[[1]](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/10/germany-to-expand-industrial-decarbonization-support-eur-6-billion-initiative-adds-ccs)

## Key Features
- **Scope Expansion**: Extends beyond hydrogen and efficiency to explicitly include CCS/CCU projects, enabling technology openness.
- **Eligibility**: Projects must achieve binding emissions milestones (60% reduction by year 3, 90% by final contract year).
- **Funding Mechanism**: State-backed contracts cover the differential between low-carbon and fossil-based production, with preparatory bidding phase open until December 2025 and auctions in mid-2026.
- **Budget and Timeline**: Part of a broader €50 billion CCfD framework launched in 2023; new €6 billion tranche focuses on 2026+ deployments with EU state-aid approval pending.

## Relevance to Industrial Decarbonization
This update strengthens Germany's position in hard-to-abate sectors by de-risking capital-intensive CCS infrastructure alongside fuel switching and efficiency measures. It aligns with EU-wide efforts like the Industrial Decarbonisation Bank and addresses competitiveness concerns amid volatile energy prices.
